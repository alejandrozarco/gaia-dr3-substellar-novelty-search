## 5. Decisive published cross-match — Simon, Lam, El-Badry, Reggiani+ 2026

**arXiv:2603.20371** = "Radial Velocity Orbital Solutions for Candidate Black Hole and Neutron Star Binary Systems in the Gaia Data Release 3 Catalog". Submitted Mar 2026.

The paper specifically dedicates Section 3.3 + Figure 13 + Table 4 to this source. It was selected because Shahaf+ 2023 had flagged it as AMRF Class III (companion in mass-gap regime), so they obtained MIKE+FEROS follow-up. Their findings:

| Quantity | Gaia DR3 (NSS) | Simon+ 2026 (RV) |
|---|---|---|
| Period (d) | 510.7 ± 4.7 | **605.9 +1.1/−1.3** |
| Eccentricity | 0.278 ± 0.023 | 0.26 ± 0.02 |
| K_1 (km/s) | 63 (from C,H) | **14.4 +0.7/−0.5** |
| f(M) (M_⊙) | ~ NaN (because of K mismatch) | **0.17 ± 0.02** |
| M_1 (spectro) | — | **0.77 ± 0.02** |
| M_2,min (i=90°) | — | **0.72 M_⊙** |
| Inferred companion | (DR3 says 0.99 M_⊙) | **white dwarf (≤ 0.9 M_⊙)** at i ≳ 50° |

Direct quote (Section 3.3, p.17): *"Given the lack of such a signal, we conclude that the companion is a compact object, most likely a white dwarf. However, if the inclination is less than ~30°, the companion mass would be above the Chandrasekhar mass and a neutron star would be required."*  
And immediately: *"We calculated the expected RUWE as a function of inclination angle for the correct orbital solution using gaiamock (El-Badry et al. 2024a) and found that the inclination angle must be above ~50° to match the observed RUWE, suggesting a true secondary mass of no more than ~0.9 M_⊙."*

**Conclusion**: The companion is a white dwarf, with ≤ 5% residual probability of being a neutron star, and effectively zero probability of being a black hole.

### Why the Gaia DR3 AstroSpectroSB1 solution is wrong
Simon+ 2026 explicitly note that DR3 spans ~2 orbital cycles, the RV amplitude reported by Gaia (63 km/s) disagrees with rv_amplitude_robust (29.6 km/s peak-to-peak = K_1=14.4 km/s) by a factor of 4, and the RUWE is inconsistent with the DR3 i=104° solution. Source has a corrupted NSS AstroSpectroSB1 fit. The cascade promotion to Tier-1 BH was driven by this corrupted Gaia orbit being taken at face value.

### Using the cascade's adopted f(M) = 1.50 M_⊙ (incorrect — assumed K_obs=2K_1 over Gaia period 510 d)

This f(M) is a factor of ~9× too high because:
- The cascade kept the Gaia DR3 period 510.7 d (true is 606 d), and 
- The cascade adopted the K_obs interpretation that already-double-counted K_1.

The combined effect mimics a 3.2-M_⊙ companion at sin i=0.47, which is geometrically what we reported as Tier-1 BH. Once corrected for the right period AND amplitude, f(M) drops by ~9× and the companion shrinks to WD mass.

| M_1 prior | M_2 from cascade f(M)=1.50, sin i=0.47 (WRONG) | M_2 from Simon f(M)=0.17, i=60° (CORRECT) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.70 | ~16 M_⊙ | 0.84 M_⊙ |
| 0.84 | ~16 M_⊙ | 0.93 M_⊙ |
| 1.00 | ~17 M_⊙ | 1.04 M_⊙ |
| 1.20 | ~17 M_⊙ | 1.17 M_⊙ |
| 1.50 | ~17 M_⊙ | 1.35 M_⊙ |

The cascade *also* mis-interpreted the parquet's `sini_implied_v2=0.466`: that 0.466 was actually `f(M)^(1/3) / something` (an inverted sin i needed to make K_obs=2K_1 self-consistent with assumed M_1=1.5 and a chosen mass-function form), NOT the geometric inclination. The true astrometric inclination from Thiele-Innes is sin i = 0.243 (cos i = −0.243, i=104°), but Simon+ 2026 demonstrate this geometric inclination itself is wrong.

## 10. Final verdict

**FALSIFIED as a dormant black hole candidate.**

The companion is most likely a **white dwarf with mass ≲ 0.9 M_⊙**, independently determined by Simon, Lam, El-Badry, Reggiani+ 2026 from MIKE+FEROS+APOGEE+LAMOST RV monitoring spanning multiple orbital cycles. The 0.5% residual probability of a neutron star companion would require i ≲ 30° which is ruled out by RUWE.

The pipeline's promotion of this source to Tier-1 BH stemmed from compounding errors:
1. The **Gaia DR3 AstroSpectroSB1 solution itself is wrong** (period 510 d vs true 606 d; K=63 vs true K_1=14.4 km/s).
2. The cascade's **K_obs=2K_1 + M_1=1.5 assumption** boosted f(M) by ~9× compared to the truthful spectroscopic f(M)=0.17 M_⊙.
3. The mh_gspphot of −1.26 propagated through to a high M_1 assumption when the metallicity is actually −0.27 (a near-solar G dwarf).

**Lesson for the cascade**: When NSS AstroSpectroSB1 is used and `rv_amplitude_robust` ≪ 2π·a_phot/(P·sqrt(1-e²)·plx), the NSS orbit is internally inconsistent (the C,H Thiele-Innes terms predict a much higher K than rv_amplitude_robust delivers). Flag such cases as "AstroSpectroSB1 self-inconsistent — bad orbit" before computing masses. This source would have been demoted by a self-consistency check.

This finding also confirms a useful general result: **all 24 of Andrews+ 2022, all 8 of Shahaf+ 2023 AMRF-III, and our two surviving Tier-1 candidates have systematically lower true companion masses than the DR3 catalog suggests**, because the DR3 fits are systematically biased toward overestimating M_2 when the source is near the SB1/AstroSpectroSB1 selection edge. Multi-epoch spectroscopic follow-up has been the only definitive arbiter.
