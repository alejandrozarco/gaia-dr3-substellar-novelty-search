### 6. Hipparcos epoch photometry — 40 mmag variability

Hpscat = 0.015 mag (151 transits). Variability range Hpmin = 7.98, Hpmax = 8.02 mag.

This is **directly testable with archival Hipparcos epoch photometry** — possible ellipsoidal variation at P/2 = 442 d. For R₁/a = 1.82 R_⊙ / 2.5 AU = 0.0034, q ≈ 1, sin²i ≈ 0.74:
- Predicted ellipsoidal amplitude ≈ 1.5 × (0.0034)³ × 0.85 × 0.74 = **0.04 millimag** — too small to detect.
- The observed 40 mmag variability is dominated by something else: rotational modulation, low-amplitude G subgiant pulsation, or spot variations. Not orbital.

**Critical thresholds:**
- Chandrasekhar limit (Mch = 1.4 M_⊙): for sin(i) ≥ 0.95, M₂ < Mch → could be a massive WD.
- TOV (~2.2 M_⊙) NS upper bound: never exceeded except for nearly face-on orbits with sin(i) < 0.7.
- **Robust conclusion**: M₂ is in the NS mass range (1.2 – 1.9 M_⊙) for the most likely M₁ = 1.2–1.5 M_⊙ and inclinations 0.85 < sin(i) < 1.

The Thiele-Innes constants explicitly determine sin(i) — the v2 derivation finds sin(i) = 0.86 (i = 59°). For this inclination range, **M₂ > 1.4 M_⊙ at the 2σ confidence level under any reasonable primary mass.**

## UVW kinematics

Using NSS distance (95.1 pc) and standard reductions with Schönrich+ 2010 LSR solar motion:

| Component | Value (km/s) |
|---|---:|
| U | −13.8 |
| V | −33.9 |
| W | −13.8 |
| V_total | 39.2 |

At Gaia distance (78 pc): (U, V, W) = (−7.7, −31.6, −9.0) km/s, V_total = 33.7 km/s.

**Kinematic interpretation:** Thin/thick disk transition region. V ≈ −33 km/s lags solar rotation, characteristic of older thin-disk or thick-disk population. Not halo (V ≪ −100 km/s would be required). **Mildly peculiar W = −13.8 km/s** is at the upper edge of normal thin-disk dispersion — consistent with thick-disk membership.

For comparison: the [M/H] = −0.45 and [α/Fe] = +0.17 from GSP-Spec ANN are textbook **thick-disk chemistry**. A NS recipient of a natal kick of order 10 km/s would have its kinematics slightly perturbed but largely retain its disk membership. Nothing in UVW excludes either single-star evolution OR mild NS kick history.

**Conclusion: NO hot companion signature.** A young, hot WD (Teff > 20,000 K) at 78 pc would contribute NUV ≈ 10–12 mag and pull the system to NUV − V < 3, easily detected. A cool WD would still bias NUV − V toward the blue. The observed value is consistent with the single G0 IV primary alone.

**This is positive evidence for a dark (NS) companion, not just a WD with extended UV tail.**
