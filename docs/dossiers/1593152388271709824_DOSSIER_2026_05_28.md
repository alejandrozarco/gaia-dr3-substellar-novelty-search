# Gaia DR3 1593152388271709824 — Tier-2 NS/WD candidate with MARGINAL 2-channel archival corroboration

*Compiled: 2026-05-28. **Updated 2026-05-28** with the full 4-epoch archival-RV set (see DR5-epoch note below) and a re-assessed verdict. Status: **highest-significance Tier-2 candidate** in the BB2 archival-RV phase consistency check. The Gaia DR3 NSS `Orbital` (astrometric-only) solution (P = 597.4 d, e = 0.404, **significance = 104.5**) is now tested against **4 archival RV epochs** (1 LAMOST LRS 2016 + 1 APOGEE DR17 2017 + 2 LAMOST MRS 2023) spanning **3 distinct orbital-phase bins**. The NSS-locked Keplerian fit gives **K_1 = 17.5 ± 5.9 km/s, γ = −22.8 km/s, ω ≈ 353°, χ²/dof = 0.002** (4 points, 3 free params, dof = 1). The DR5/LRS epoch near periastron is **consistent** with the orbit (0.37σ from the extrapolation of the 3 high-precision epochs) and the spectroscopic mass function f(M)_RV = 0.26 M_⊙ now **agrees with the astrometric f_phot = 0.29 M_⊙** at < 0.2σ — i.e. the K–ω degeneracy is broken and the two channels corroborate. **However, K_1 is detected at only ~3σ (2.95σ linear / 3.12σ MC) because the entire amplitude rests on a single low-S/N LRS epoch (HRV = −0.68 ± 10.54 km/s).** This is therefore a **MARGINAL_2CHANNEL** corroboration — upgraded from WEAK, but short of a robust > 3σ CONFIRMED. Primary is a G subgiant (Teff ≈ 5800 K, logg ≈ 3.95, M ≈ 1.4 M_⊙, R ≈ 2.65 R_⊙) at d ≈ 469 pc. At M_1 = 1.40 M_⊙ (FLAME), the tight astrometric channel gives **M_2 = 1.27 ± 0.04 M_⊙** (WD/NS boundary), while the loose dynamical channel gives M_2 = 1.28 (+0.69/−0.52) M_⊙ with **P(NS) ≈ 42%, P(BH) ≈ 2%**.*

## 0. UPDATE NOTE — the LAMOST "DR5" epoch (2026-05-28 re-analysis)

A previous compilation run was interrupted at the moment it flagged a LAMOST DR5 RV record as a potential **3rd distinct orbital phase**. That record has now been fully resolved, with two important corrections:

1. **The epoch is real but mis-dated in the interrupted note.** LAMOST DR5 LRS (Vizier `V/164/stellar5`) **ObsID 455703189** has **HRV = −0.68 ± 10.54 km/s**. The interrupted note read the PlanId `HD145818N500615V01` as encoding "2015-06-15". That is wrong: the PlanId encodes the **plate/field-center coordinates** (HD145818N500615 → field centred at RA ≈ 14h58m18s, Dec ≈ +50°06′15″), not a date. The archive `Obs.Date` for this observation is **2016-04-23, MJD = 57501** (confirmed in both `V/164/stellar5` and `V/156/dr7lrs`).

2. **"DR5 epoch" = the single LAMOST low-resolution epoch; it is NOT a 5th observation.** The same ObsID 455703189 appears in LAMOST DR5 and DR7 LRS catalogs as a single LRS visit. Separately, the "**2 LAMOST DR11 epochs**" referenced in the original draft are **medium-resolution (MRS)** coadds taken in 2023 (`V/162/dr11sm`): MObsID 1125607216 (MJD 60096, 2023-06-01) and 1126507216 (MJD 60101, 2023-06-06) — only **5 days apart**, i.e. effectively the same orbital phase. The complete archival RV set is therefore **4 epochs across 3 distinct phase bins** (LRS 2016 near periastron, APOGEE 2017, MRS 2023 ×2).

**Net effect on the verdict:** the LRS epoch *does* break the K–ω degeneracy (it lands at phase φ = 0.04, near periastron, ~0.3–0.7 in phase from the other epochs) and *is* consistent with the NSS Keplerian, upgrading the corroboration from WEAK to **MARGINAL_2CHANNEL**. But because the LRS RV error is ±10.54 km/s, the resulting K_1 = 17.5 km/s is only a ~3σ detection — not the robust > 3σ needed for CONFIRMED. Full re-analysis in **§6** and **§9**. Raw queries saved to `/tmp/ns_1593152_*.json`.

**Critical caveat — MSC suggests a luminous binary.** The Gaia DR3 MSC (Multi-Stellar Classifier) fit returns `logposterior_msc = 684.8` (substantially > 0, with a 2-component solution: 5901 K + 5714 K, both with logg > 4.2). **A luminous binary fit with two G/K dwarfs is preferred over a single-star fit** at very high statistical confidence in the Gaia DR3 photometric+astrometric likelihood. However:

1. **The single-star FLAME fit at M = 1.40, R = 2.65 R_⊙ is internally consistent** with the APOGEE spectroscopic Teff and logg.
2. **Two G dwarfs at logg ≈ 4.2 and logg ≈ 4.6** (the MSC fit) would imply M_1 ≈ 0.95-1.10 M_⊙ and M_2 ≈ 0.65-0.80 M_⊙ — a normal SB2 G+K dwarf binary, NOT a compact-object system. **This is the principal demotion risk for this candidate.**
3. **The NSS Orbital fit (significance = 104.5) does NOT incorporate the MSC two-component decomposition**; it treats the system as a single photocenter with an unmodelled astrometric wobble. The astrometric wobble is real (a_phot ≈ 0.9 mas; see §4), but its interpretation depends on whether the secondary contributes significant flux.
4. **Critically: APOGEE high-resolution spectroscopy returns Vsini = 6.98 km/s, single template, and Chi² = 3.08** — no SB2 line-doubling is reported in the ASPCAP fit, and the SNR = 232 visit shows a normal single G3IV spectrum. This is the strongest single argument against the MSC luminous-binary interpretation.
5. **`phot_bp_rp_excess_factor = 1.198`** is only mildly elevated (single-star envelope at this BP-RP is ~1.10-1.20), inconsistent with a strong luminous-binary blend.

**Resolution:** Either (a) the MSC fit is over-fitting a normal subgiant photometric SED with a degenerate two-component decomposition (a known DR3 systematic), OR (b) the secondary is a G/K dwarf that contributes ~30% of the optical flux but has CCF lines so close to the primary that APOGEE 22500-resolution did not deblend them in a single visit. Option (a) is favoured by the clean single-template APOGEE fit. **Option (b) cannot be definitively ruled out without (i) phase-resolved APOGEE/CHIRON/NEID epochs or (ii) a re-fit of MSC with appropriate priors.**

**Activity / age:**
- APOGEE Vsini = 6.98 km/s suggests moderate rotation, consistent with a young subgiant (≤ 3 Gyr).
- No GCVS variable flag; no AAVSO long-term photometric variability detected.
- AllWISE W1/W2 var flag = 3/2 — **W1/W2 photometric variability is flagged**, but variability at this WISE-photometric precision is typical for active G dwarfs and does not unambiguously indicate binarity or compact-object accretion.

**a_phot — semi-major axis of the photocentric orbit (Pourbaix-Halbwachs formalism):**

The astrometric Thiele-Innes elements A, B, F, G fully determine the photocentric orbit shape and orientation. Following Halbwachs 2023 eq. 6, we compute:
- u = A² + B² + F² + G² = 4.1248 mas²
- v = AG − BF = 1.0944 mas²
- **a_phot = sqrt[(u + sqrt(u² − 4v²))/2] = 1.9521 mas** (central)
- **a_phot (MC 16/50/84%) = 1.930 / 1.954 / 1.979 mas**
- **b_phot / a_phot = cos i = 0.287** → **sin i central = 0.958**
- **MC sin i (16/50/84%) = 0.952 / 0.958 / 0.963**
- **Inferred inclination i (16/50/84%) = 72.2° / 73.3° / 74.5°**
- **a_phot in AU** (using NSS parallax 2.1307 mas): **0.904 / 0.917 / 0.931 AU (16/50/84%)**

If the MSC luminous-binary interpretation is correct, the photocenter wobble would be attenuated; the implied dynamical secondary mass for a 0.6-0.7 M_⊙ K dwarf companion at the observed a_phot is much smaller and consistent.

**Inclination interpretation:** The inferred i = 73.3° (median) is moderately edge-on; sin i ≈ 0.96. This is fortunate for both transit geometry (R_1/a = 0.013 — transit probability 0.5%, geometrically expected non-detection) and RV semi-amplitude (K_1 ∝ sin i).

### 6.3 NSS-locked Keplerian fit (K_1, γ, ω free; P, e, T_peri fixed)

RV model: `RV(t) = γ + K_1·[cos(ν + ω) + e·cos ω]`, fit by Levenberg–Marquardt; uncertainties from the linearised covariance and from a Monte-Carlo that propagates the NSS (P, e, T_peri) errors and the per-epoch RV noise (N = 20 000).

```
   K_1   = 17.49 ± 5.93 km/s        (linear S/N = 2.95; MC |K| 16/50/84 = 11.9 / 17.5 / 23.1, median/σ = 3.12)
   γ     = −22.80 ± 2.89 km/s
   ω     = 353.5° ± 1.3°
   χ²    = 0.002,  dof = 1  (4 points − 3 free params),  χ²/dof = 0.002
```

| Epoch | predicted RV | observed RV | residual (σ) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| LAMOST LRS | −0.74 | −0.68 ± 10.54 | −0.005 |
| APOGEE DR17 | −30.97 | −30.967 ± 0.040 | +0.000 |
| LAMOST MRS ep1 | −31.49 | −31.50 ± 0.42 | +0.030 |
| LAMOST MRS ep2 | −31.68 | −31.67 ± 0.43 | −0.031 |

**The 4-epoch fit is excellent (χ²/dof = 0.002), but with dof = 1 the χ² value alone is a weak statistic** — 3 free parameters fit to 4 points will almost always give a tiny χ². The meaningful statements are the two below.

**(a) The constant-RV (no-orbit) null is rejected.** Fitting all 4 epochs to a constant gives χ² = 12.47 for dof = 3 (p ≈ 0.006, ≈ 2.7σ). The 30 km/s LRS excursion is not consistent with a static γ. (The 3 high-precision epochs *alone* about a constant give χ² = 4.21 / dof = 2, χ²/dof = 2.1 — marginal — so the rejection of the constant is driven by the LRS point.)

**(b) The LRS epoch is consistent with the orbit, not an outlier.** Taking the *three high-precision* epochs to fix the (K_1, γ, ω) family (which is degenerate, dof = 0, because they sample only 2 phases) and **extrapolating to the LRS phase** predicts RV(φ = 0.042) = **−4.5 km/s (central), with a 2.5–97.5% MC range of −9.3 to +0.2 km/s**. The observed LRS RV of −0.68 ± 10.54 km/s lies inside this predicted family at **0.37σ**. → The DR5/LRS epoch **matches the Keplerian prediction**; this rules out an INCONSISTENT verdict (no evidence for a triple or a wrong period).

**(c) K_1 is detected at only ~3σ.** The MC gives P(|K_1| < 3 km/s) = 0.3%, i.e. |K_1| > 3 km/s at ≈ 99.7% (≈ 3σ), and median/σ = 3.12. The linear covariance gives K_1/σ = 2.95. **The detection straddles the 3σ line and rests almost entirely on the single ±10.54 km/s LRS epoch** — the three sub-km/s epochs span only 0.7 km/s over 2 nearby phases and cannot by themselves pin K_1.

## 9. Verdict + RV follow-up proposal

### 9.1 Verdict: **MARGINAL_2CHANNEL** (upgraded from WEAK_CORROBORATING)

| Decision criterion | Result | Met? |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 3 distinct orbital-phase bins populated | **3** (φ = 0.04, 0.39, 0.70) | **YES** — the DR5/LRS epoch supplies the 3rd (periastron) bin |
| χ²/dof ≤ 2 for the NSS-locked fit | **0.002** (dof = 1) | YES (but dof = 1 → weak statistic) |
| K_1 constrained at > 3σ | **2.95σ (linear) / 3.12σ (MC)** | **MARGINAL** — straddles 3σ; rests on one ±10.54 km/s epoch |
| DR5 epoch consistent with the NSS Keplerian | **0.37σ** from the 3-HP extrapolation | YES — *not* an outlier (rules out INCONSISTENT / triple) |
| Constant-RV null rejected | χ² = 12.47 / dof = 3 (≈ 2.7σ) | YES |
| Two mass functions agree | f(M)_RV = 0.26 vs f_phot = 0.29 (< 0.2σ) | YES |

**Reasoning for the intermediate verdict.** The newly-resolved DR5/LRS epoch does the qualitative job the original dossier hoped for: it populates a **third, well-separated phase bin near periastron**, it is **consistent** with the NSS orbit (0.37σ), and it pulls the spectroscopic mass function into **quantitative agreement** with the astrometric f_phot. By the letter of the rubric this satisfies "3+ distinct phases AND χ²/dof ≤ 2", so the K–ω degeneracy is **broken** and the system is genuinely **2-channel corroborated**. 

**But it is not a robust CONFIRMED.** The strict CONFIRMED_2CHANNEL bar requires K_1 constrained at > 3σ, and the detection is only ≈ 3σ (2.95σ linear, 3.12σ MC) because the entire RV amplitude is carried by a **single low-S/N LAMOST LRS epoch with ±10.54 km/s error**. The three sub-km/s epochs alone span 0.7 km/s over two nearby phases and are individually consistent with a constant at χ²/dof = 2.1. Remove or down-weight the LRS point and the orbital RV detection largely evaporates. This is materially weaker than the 3155543 CONFIRMED case, where two **3.6 km/s-precision** LAMOST epochs at well-separated phases gave a ΔRV = +13.7 km/s detected at > 3σ from precise data.

**This is therefore classified MARGINAL_2CHANNEL: the degeneracy is broken and both channels agree, but the confirmation hinges on one noisy epoch and is ≈ 3σ, not the clean > 3σ of a CONFIRMED NS.** It is **not** the project's 3rd independent-2-channel-confirmed NS; it is a strong Tier-2 candidate that is one precise epoch away from a definitive call.

**Most likely physical nature.** M_2 ≈ 1.27 M_⊙ from both channels places the companion at the WD/NS boundary. At face value (tight astrometric channel, dark-companion limit) the most probable interpretation is a **massive (near-Chandrasekhar) white dwarf**; a low-mass neutron star is allowed at the ≈ 42% level by the dynamical channel and cannot be excluded. A black-hole companion is disfavoured (≈ 2%). The residual demotion risk is the MSC luminous-binary alternative (§3, §7): if a ~0.6–0.7 M_⊙ K dwarf contributes significant optical flux, the photocentre wobble is attenuated and the true M_2 is lower (a normal SB2), though the clean single-template APOGEE spectrum argues against this.

### 9.2 Follow-up that would settle it (single epoch)

The orbit is now fully predictive. T_peri = MJD 57476.05, P = 597.36 d → future periastra at **MJD 60463 (2024-06-01)**, **MJD 61060 (2026-01-20)**, **MJD 61658 (2027-09-09)** (± ~10 d from P, T_peri errors). The fitted curve (K_1 ≈ 17.5, γ ≈ −22.8, ω ≈ 353°) predicts:

| Target phase | Predicted RV (km/s) | Why |
|---|---:|---|
| φ ≈ 0.0 (periastron, e.g. near MJD 61060 = 2026-01-20) | **≈ 0 to +3** | maximum excursion from the −31 km/s cluster; ~30 km/s swing |
| φ ≈ 0.5 (apastron) | ≈ −33 to −35 | opposite extreme |

**A single 0.1–0.5 km/s-precision epoch (NEID, HARPS-N, TRES, FEROS) taken within ±30 d of periastron would measure the ~30 km/s amplitude at > 50σ**, pin K_1 to a few %, and convert this MARGINAL case into a definitive CONFIRMED (or refute it). Because the predicted periastron RV (≈ 0 km/s) is ~30 km/s from the well-sampled −31 km/s cluster, even a modest-precision (~1 km/s) spectrograph suffices. This is the **highest-value single follow-up epoch** of any Tier-2 target in the sample.
