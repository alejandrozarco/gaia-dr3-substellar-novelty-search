**Critical caveat — MSC suggests a luminous binary.** The Gaia DR3 MSC (Multi-Stellar Classifier) fit returns `logposterior_msc = 684.8` (substantially > 0, with a 2-component solution: 5901 K + 5714 K, both with logg > 4.2). **A luminous binary fit with two G/K dwarfs is preferred over a single-star fit** at very high statistical confidence in the Gaia DR3 photometric+astrometric likelihood. However:

1. **The single-star FLAME fit at M = 1.40, R = 2.65 R_⊙ is internally consistent** with the APOGEE spectroscopic Teff and logg.
2. **Two G dwarfs at logg ≈ 4.2 and logg ≈ 4.6** (the MSC fit) would imply M_1 ≈ 0.95-1.10 M_⊙ and M_2 ≈ 0.65-0.80 M_⊙ — a normal SB2 G+K dwarf binary, NOT a compact-object system. **This is the principal demotion risk for this candidate.**
3. **The NSS Orbital fit (significance = 104.5) does NOT incorporate the MSC two-component decomposition**; it treats the system as a single photocenter with an unmodelled astrometric wobble. The astrometric wobble is real (a_phot ≈ 0.9 mas; see §4), but its interpretation depends on whether the secondary contributes significant flux.
4. **Critically: APOGEE high-resolution spectroscopy returns Vsini = 6.98 km/s, single template, and Chi² = 3.08** — no SB2 line-doubling is reported in the ASPCAP fit, and the SNR = 232 visit shows a normal single G3IV spectrum. This is the strongest single argument against the MSC luminous-binary interpretation.
5. **`phot_bp_rp_excess_factor = 1.198`** is only mildly elevated (single-star envelope at this BP-RP is ~1.10-1.20), inconsistent with a strong luminous-binary blend.

**Resolution:** Either (a) the MSC fit is over-fitting a normal subgiant photometric SED with a degenerate two-component decomposition (a known DR3 systematic), OR (b) the secondary is a G/K dwarf that contributes ~30% of the optical flux but has CCF lines so close to the primary that APOGEE 22500-resolution did not deblend them in a single visit. Option (a) is favoured by the clean single-template APOGEE fit. **Option (b) cannot be definitively ruled out without (i) phase-resolved APOGEE/CHIRON/NEID epochs or (ii) a re-fit of MSC with appropriate priors.**

**Activity / age:**
- APOGEE Vsini = 6.98 km/s suggests moderate rotation, consistent with a young subgiant (≤ 3 Gyr).
- No GCVS variable flag; no AAVSO long-term photometric variability detected.
- AllWISE W1/W2 var flag = 3/2 — **W1/W2 photometric variability is flagged**, but variability at this WISE-photometric precision is typical for active G dwarfs and does not unambiguously indicate binarity or compact-object accretion.

**a_phot — semi-major axis of the photocentric orbit (Pourbaix-Halbwachs formalism):**

The astrometric Thiele-Innes elements A, B, F, G fully determine the photocentric orbit shape and orientation. Following Halbwachs 2023 eq. 6, we compute:
- u = A² + B² + F² + G² = 4.1248 mas²
- v = AG − BF = 1.0944 mas²
- **a_phot = sqrt[(u + sqrt(u² − 4v²))/2] = 1.9521 mas** (central)
- **a_phot (MC 16/50/84%) = 1.930 / 1.954 / 1.979 mas**
- **b_phot / a_phot = cos i = 0.287** → **sin i central = 0.958**
- **MC sin i (16/50/84%) = 0.952 / 0.958 / 0.963**
- **Inferred inclination i (16/50/84%) = 72.2° / 73.3° / 74.5°**
- **a_phot in AU** (using NSS parallax 2.1307 mas): **0.904 / 0.917 / 0.931 AU (16/50/84%)**

**This is a very strong dark-companion candidate from the astrometric mass function alone.** The astrometric posterior gives a substantial probability of stellar-mass BH territory (M_2 > 3 M_⊙), with much of the weight on NS (1.4-2.2 M_⊙). At face value, this candidate is *more likely to be a BH than a NS at the adopted M_1.*

**Caveat on the dark-companion assumption.** The astrometric f_phot is *not* equivalent to the dynamical f(M) in the dark-companion limit — it equals the dynamical f(M) *only if the secondary contributes zero optical flux*. If the secondary is a 0.7 M_⊙ K dwarf (the MSC two-component fit alternative), the photocenter wobble is *attenuated* by the flux ratio, and the inferred M_2 from f_phot can be substantially overestimated. The most stringent test of the dark-companion assumption is the §6 archival RV analysis — if the primary is a single star and the secondary is dark, the systemic velocity γ should be constant across visits and the RV variation should follow the predicted Keplerian.

**Photometric subgiant + astrometric f_phot ≈ 0.31 M_⊙ → secondary mass ≈ 1.3 M_⊙** at adopted M_1. If the MSC luminous-binary interpretation is correct, the photocenter wobble would be attenuated; the implied dynamical secondary mass for a 0.6-0.7 M_⊙ K dwarf companion at the observed a_phot is much smaller and consistent.

**Inclination interpretation:** The inferred i = 73.3° (median) is moderately edge-on; sin i ≈ 0.96. This is fortunate for both transit geometry (R_1/a = 0.013 — transit probability 0.5%, geometrically expected non-detection) and RV semi-amplitude (K_1 ∝ sin i).
