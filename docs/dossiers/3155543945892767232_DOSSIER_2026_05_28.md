**Expected ellipsoidal modulation (Morris-Gomel):**
- a_total = (G(M_1+M_2) P²/4π²)^(1/3) = **1.87 AU = 401 R_⊙** (M_1 = 1.4, M_2 = 1.26)
- R_1 / a = **0.0108**
- q = M_2/M_1 = 0.75 (taking M_2 = 1.05 for an NS)
- α_1 = 1.5 (limb-darkening)
- **A_ell (sin² i = 1) ≈ 1 ppm**
- A_ell (sin² i = 0.69, i = 56°) ≈ 0.7 ppm

**Verdict: predicted ellipsoidal amplitude (~1 ppm) is THREE orders of magnitude below TESS noise floor (~3 600 ppm/cadence). TESS cannot test the orbit via ellipsoidal modulation.** The observed LS power at P/2 (7.7e-3) is consistent with TESS systematic/red-noise at the 100-ppm/27-d-period scale and is not a real binary signal.

**Transit non-detection:** at a = 1.87 AU and R_1 = 4.2 R_⊙, transit probability is R_1/a ≈ 1.0%. Non-detection is geometrically expected and uninformative.

**Reflection / heating:** for a NS or WD companion, expected reflection is zero (compact, dark). For a luminous secondary K-dwarf scenario at R_2 ~ 0.7 R_⊙ at a = 1.87 AU, reflection amplitude is (R_2/2a)² ~ 3 × 10⁻⁷ ~ 0.3 ppm — also below detection threshold.

### 9.4 Risk factors

1. **AstroSpectroSB1 class has substantial systematic uncertainties.** Halbwachs+ 2023 noted that the spectroscopic Thiele-Innes elements C, H are not always cleanly recoverable in the DR3 release due to the limited number of RV transits per source. For this target, 12 transits with rv_expected_sig_to_noise = 20.4 represents a reasonable but not extraordinary sample. The fact that the LAMOST archival 2-epoch ΔRV is consistent with the NSS-predicted orbit at the right phases is the strongest single argument that the orbit is real.

2. **GALEX null does not rule out a cooled WD.** A WD with Teff < 20 000 K at d = 1 kpc would be GALEX-undetected. The 38% astrometric probability of M_2 < 1.4 M_⊙ should not be dismissed.

3. **Müller-Horn+ 2026's flag_quality = False** is a soft negative. Their fit was based only on Gaia RVs (no LAMOST), and their orbital period was misidentified (1014 d vs the NSS 543 d). Re-running their analysis with the NSS prior would tighten their constraint substantially.

4. **NSS plx and pm differ from gaia_source by 3-5σ.** This is *expected* for a strong photocentre orbital signal at this period — the NSS solution properly accounts for the orbital motion that biases the single-source fit. But this is also a source of potential systematic if the NSS fit is itself contaminated.

5. **The Pourbaix convention for the AstroSpectroSB1 ω value is non-standard.** The 90° rotation needed to match the LAMOST data is consistent with the documented Gaia DR3 NSS conventions but represents a known issue with combining astrometric and spectroscopic Thiele-Innes elements. The MAP fit (free ω) provides a clean test: ω is well-constrained at ~171° with very small chi² of 0.36.

**This is the strongest two-channel-corroborated NS candidate in the v2-relaxed catalog.** The Gaia DR3 AstroSpectroSB1 orbit at P = 543 d, e = 0.09 is independently corroborated by LAMOST LRS 2-epoch +13.67 km/s ΔRV at the correct orbital phases with χ² = 0.36. M_2 = 1.26 M_⊙ from the v2-relaxed expansion is at the heavy-WD / light-NS border with 62% probability of being super-Chandrasekhar at the adopted M_1 = 1.4 M_⊙.
