## 5. TESS coverage

**37 sectors** of TESS coverage between Sector 2 (Aug 2018) and Sector 98 (Oct 2024) = **2393 d baseline = 9.4 orbital cycles at P = 253.5 d**. SPOC 2-minute LCs available for 30+ sectors; 20-second cadence for a subset (S35, S37, S39).

**This work — stitched LC of 30 sectors (N = 472,885 photometric points):**

| Metric | Value | Notes |
|---|---:|---|
| Total baseline | 2393 d | |
| Out-of-eclipse RMS (no detrending) | 1671 ppm | typical for a quiet M4V |
| Phase-folded P2P at P_NSS = 253.5 d (50 bins) | 187 ppm | no transit signature |
| Phase-folded P2P at P_NSS/2 = 126.7 d | 199 ppm | no ellipsoidal variation |
| BLS search ±3% around P_NSS, depths to 2000 ppm | depth = 2161 ppm at P = 258.4 d | marginal — likely systematics, not real |
| Lomb-Scargle dominant period | **10.51 d** | rotational modulation |

**Rotation:** the 10.51 d LS peak is consistent with the field-age rotation expected for an M4V (Newton+ 2016 calibration: 10-30 d for mature M4 dwarfs). This rules out spot-induced activity-cycle artefacts at the 253 d orbital period (factor of ~24× separation).

**Transit non-detection:** for the predicted a_total = 0.53 AU at M_1 = 0.25 M_⊙, transit probability is R_1/a = 0.3 R_⊙ / 0.53 AU = **0.26%** for circular orbit (slightly higher at e = 0.13 if periastron is closer). The 37-sector LC would have caught any ~5-10% transit dip at P = 253 d, but a single 10 M_J planet has a planetary radius of ~1.0-1.2 R_J = 0.10-0.12 R_⊙, giving an expected depth of (0.1 R_⊙/0.30 R_⊙)² = 11% if it transited. **Non-detection is geometric:** the orbit is almost certainly non-transiting.

**Variability scan:** no eclipse, no transit, no >0.05% phase modulation at P or P/2 → companion is NOT a stellar M dwarf (which would produce ~0.5-2% ellipsoidal modulation at P/2 given a/R_1 = 0.53*215/0.30 = 380, so ellipsoidal depth (R_1/a)³ q ~ 10^-7 — negligible). **The companion is dark / substellar.**

### Number of nights to detect K_1 at 5σ

For K_1 ≈ 800 m/s and σ_RV ≈ 5 m/s (CHIRON), 5σ amplitude requires σ_K1 = 160 m/s. With a 253-day orbit, **3-4 RV epochs spanning ~150 days** are sufficient. With FEROS or HARPS at 5-10 m/s, **only 3 epochs** are needed for >10σ detection. **One ~5-night campaign (e.g., 5 epochs over 6 months) settles the question definitively.**

### Risk factors

1. **Stellar activity:** the 10.5 d rotation period is well-separated from the 253-d orbital period and its harmonics; RV jitter from spots typically <30 m/s for old M4V → at most a ~5% systematic, easily absorbed.
2. **Single-line spectrum issue:** for M4V at d = 17 pc the K-band TiO/CO features are well-resolved at modern echelle resolutions; CHIRON's iodine cell and ThAr modes both work well. The companion at q ≈ 0.05 is far from the SB2 transition (the Wilson regime is fine — the M_2 light is buried at flux ratio ~10^-3 even for a brown-dwarf companion).
3. **Inclination from astrometry vs RV:** combining a_phot (astrometry) + K_1 (RV) + P + e fully determines i and (independently) M_2. This is the gold standard for orbital mass measurement.
4. **Trojan / aliasing:** the 10.5 d rotation is incommensurable with 253.5 d (ratio = 24.1, no low-order resonance). Safe.
5. **Activity-induced K-band jitter:** ~50-100 m/s peak for old M4V (Reiners+ 2010); subdominant to predicted K_1.
