# HD 173689 — Archival dossier

**Date**: 2026-05-28
**Source pool**: HGCA Brandt 2021 chi²>25 ∧ no-Gaia-DR3 NSS solution (HGCA PMa Hunt 2026-05-28, brightest novel candidate at G=7.16).
**Verdict (headline)**: **DEMOTED — known resolved hierarchical triple via WDS (Ba,Bb pair at 1.0″ + BC pair at 7.1″) and a Gaia DR3 companion at 7.37″ (G=10.98). Kervella 2022 H2G2 returns snrPMaH2G2 = 25.79 and M₂(5 AU) = 2056 M_J ≈ 1.96 M☉ — i.e. an early-A or late-B inner stellar companion at a few AU. The HGCA chi² = 2269 is gigantic precisely because the system is a known close visual multiple, NOT because there is a BH. Demote with a clean "known-multiple" explanation.**

**Be-star check**: no BeSS spectra hit; no Reed 2003 hit; no Jaschek-Egret 1982 hit. The SIMBAD `otype` is plain `*`, not `Be*`. SIMBAD `sp_type` is "B9" without an "e" suffix.

**IR excess**: K-W3 = 8.996 − 8.397 = **+0.60 mag**, K-W4 = 8.996 − 8.141 = **+0.86 mag**. These are non-trivial mid-IR excesses — for a B9V SED, K-W3 and K-W4 should both be near 0 mag. A Be shell at the warm-dust / decretion-disc end would produce ~1–3 mag of W3/W4 excess; the observed 0.6–0.9 mag is *consistent with* either a marginal Be disc OR a hot dusty companion / debris belt. **This is a soft Be-star caveat — not a Be confirmation, but worth flagging.**

**vsini**: not in Glebocki & Gnacinski (J/A+A/450/735) — but at G=7.2 and SpT B9, a published vsini is plausible in other catalogs (Royer et al. catalogs of A-star rotation may have it; not queried here in the time budget).

**Verdict on Be contamination**: NOT confirmed Be, but soft IR excess (+0.6–0.9 mag in W3/W4) is a marginal warning. If a Be disc IS present, the HGCA chi² could in principle be inflated by photocentric jitter from the disc emission. However, this would not explain the 25.79-σ Kervella H2G2 signal (see §3), which is a 25-yr-baseline geometric acceleration, not an instantaneous photocentric perturbation.

## 4. Why NOT in DR3 NSS

- Gaia DR3 NSS query (via Vizier I/357 family): no entry in any orbital, acceleration, or non-linear spectro table.
- RUWE = 2.38 — above the "single source" threshold, which generally pushes DR3 NSS to either flag astrometric NSS or defer.
- The system's brightness (G=7.16) means Gaia operates near the bright-end calibration regime, and the **1.0″ Ba,Bb pair is partially resolved** in Gaia DR3 (the centroid of source 2091887087360991744 likely blends the unresolved Ba,Bb but the BC component at 7.1″ is fully resolved as a separate Gaia source — see §7).
- Period: at dVt = 7498 m/s and the system mass scale, a 1.0″ separation at 308 pc → projected sep ~308 AU; orbital period for a 308 AU stellar binary is ~5000 yr (way too long for NSS). But a tighter unresolved spectro inner pair (period a few years) would be the actual driver of the HGCA chi².

The hunt's "M₂_med = 4.3 M☉" was at M₁=1.5 averaged over the P-grid centered on 11.6 yr. With the correct M₁ = 3.29 (Gaia FLAME), the M₂_median shifts to ~5–6 M☉ — still nominally BH-class if you assume the PMa is from a single unresolved companion.

**But the assumption is wrong.** Kervella 2022 gives a direct mass estimate at fiducial 5 AU = ~2 M☉, which corresponds to a stellar A-type inner companion. The 5-AU orbital period for M_total ≈ 5.3 M☉ is P ≈ 5 AU^(3/2) / M^(1/2) = √(125 / 5.3) ≈ **4.9 yr** — and at P=5 yr, the table gives M₂ ≈ 3.8 M☉ (close to Kervella's ~2 M☉ within the dVt window function uncertainty). **Self-consistent solution: P ≈ 5 yr, M₂ ≈ 2–4 M☉, i.e. an A-star inner companion.**

## 7. Visual companion check (CRITICAL FOR THIS TARGET)

**WDS B/wds/wds**: **2 entries** within 30″ on WDS J18451+3500:
- **Ba,Bb**: sep₁ = 1.0″, sep₂ = 1.1″, mag1 = 9.82, mag2 = 11.30, PA = 218° — close pair (resolved by speckle imaging; barely resolved in Gaia DR3 imagery for the primary)
- **BC**: sep₁ = 7.1″, sep₂ = 7.3″, mag1 = 9.82, mag2 = 10.50, PA = 306° — wide pair

**Gaia DR3 within 10″**: 6 sources beyond the primary. Three are foreground/background faint (G > 18). Three are relevant:
- Gaia DR3 4211368888622496512: G=19.07, sep=3.20″ — likely background.
- Gaia DR3 4211368888631477760: G=**10.98**, sep=**7.37″** — **this is the WDS BC companion** (mag and separation match within 1″).
- Plus the primary itself at sep=0.

**Note that the 1.0″ Ba,Bb pair is NOT resolved as two separate Gaia DR3 sources** — the primary's G = 7.16 and any companion at ΔG ≈ +4 mag (11.30 − 7.16 ≈ 4.1) within 1.0″ would be a partial-blend that Gaia DR3 typically delivers as a single high-RUWE source. This matches our observation: RUWE = 2.38, which is consistent with a barely-unresolved companion at sub-arcsec separation.

**Interpretation**: HD 173689 is a hierarchical triple:
- **A (primary, the catalog target)** = B9III/IV at d=308 pc, M ≈ 3.3 M☉ (FLAME).
- **Bb (inner companion to A, 1.0″ ≈ 308 AU projected)** — actually the WDS calls this "Ba,Bb" pair; it should be more properly Aa,Ab. The 1.0″ separation at 308 pc = 308 AU projected, so orbital P ≈ 3000 yr — far too long to be the HGCA chi² driver. However, the WDS magnitudes (mag1=9.82, mag2=11.30) are at a different epoch and may not be the relevant geometry; **WDS often catalogs measurements made decades ago**.
- **C (the wide partner at 7.37″ ≈ 2270 AU)** — G=10.98, fully resolved as a Gaia DR3 source.

The HGCA chi² and 25.79-σ Kervella signal must come from an **unresolved-by-WDS, unresolved-by-Gaia inner companion** at separation ≪ 1″ — most likely a few AU. The Gaia DR3 RUWE = 2.38 confirms this. The Kervella M₂(5 AU) = ~2 M☉ at PA = 292° is the working model.

**Verdict**: HD 173689 is a confirmed hierarchical triple-or-quadruple system. The PMa signal is the inner unresolved stellar companion. There is no BH.

## 10. Verdict + follow-up plan

**Verdict: DEMOTED.** The HGCA chi² = 2269 is the second-highest in the top-13 list (only HD 37943 is higher) because the system is a known close visual multiple. The Kervella 2022 H2G2 25.79-σ signal at M₂(5 AU) ≈ 2 M☉ confirms an unresolved A-star inner companion at a few AU, not a BH.

Specifically:

- **WDS Ba,Bb 1.0″ + BC 7.1″** = catalogued hierarchical triple.
- **Gaia DR3 source at 7.37″ G=10.98** = independent confirmation of the WDS BC component.
- **RUWE = 2.38** confirms an additional unresolved inner companion at sub-arcsec.
- **Kervella H2G2 snr = 25.79** is an unambiguous astrometric-acceleration detection at M₂ ≈ 2 M☉, consistent with an early-A companion at P ≈ 5 yr.
- **No X-ray, no radio** — no LMXB / accreting BH.
- The "M₂ = 4.3 M☉ BH-class" hunt headline at M₁=1.5 is a default-mass artifact; with FLAME M₁ = 3.29 the median rises slightly, but every inversion assumes single-companion which the WDS + Gaia + RUWE + Kervella all collectively falsify.

**Follow-up plan (now a methodology calibration, not a discovery)**:

1. **High-resolution multi-epoch spectroscopy** (FIES/ARCES/HRS at 2-m class facilities) to measure RV. With V=7.2, σ_RV ≈ 30 m/s in a single 5-min exposure is straightforward, and a K ≈ 5–10 km/s inner stellar companion at P=5 yr is trivially detectable in ~5–10 epochs over 1 yr. This would convert HD 173689 from a astrometric-only multiple to a spectroscopic-binary catalog entry (it would then move into SB9 / Gaia DR4 NSS).
2. **Speckle imaging** to constrain the inner companion at sub-arcsec — Robo-AO / 'Alopeke / AstraLux can resolve any pair down to ~50 mas at this brightness.
3. **Gaia DR4 epoch astrometry** (Dec 2026) will resolve the inner companion via Thiele-Innes orbit fitting; the RUWE = 2.38 in DR3 should drop dramatically in DR4 when the full astrometric chain is delivered.
4. **Use as benchmark**: HD 173689 is a textbook example of "bright HGCA chi² candidate driven by known visual multiplicity". Like CD-46 10032A and HD 37419, it complements the negative-control set for the methodology paper.

**Reframing**: This was the brightest novel candidate (G=7.2). The brightness IS a virtue — it means trivial RV follow-up can confirm the inner stellar companion. The system is interesting as a **stellar-orbital benchmark**, not as a BH host.

**Operational lesson**: For HGCA chi²>1000 candidates, the WDS cone + Kervella 2022 snrPMaH2G2 + RUWE check should automatically trigger a "known multiple" branch in the candidate ranking. Adding `kervella_BinH2G2 = 1` AND `kervella_snrH2G2 > 5` to the demote logic catches both CD-46 10032A and HD 173689.
