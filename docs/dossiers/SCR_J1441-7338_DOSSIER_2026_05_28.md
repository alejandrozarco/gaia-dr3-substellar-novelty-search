### Nights to detect K_1 at 5σ

For K_1 ≈ 800 m/s and σ_RV ≈ 10 m/s (HARPS, 1-h integration), 5σ amplitude requires σ_K1 = 160 m/s → only **3 epochs are needed for >10σ**. With NIRPS at <5 m/s in NIR, **2 epochs suffice**.

**One ~3-night HARPS or NIRPS run over 6 months** would settle the question.

### Risk factors

1. **Star faintness:** G = 14.8 requires aperture-class 3.6m+ for sub-100 m/s RV precision. Below CHIRON's comfortable reach.
2. **Possible youth / activity:** the 0.83-d TESS LS peak hints at fast rotation, which would translate to **30-100 m/s of activity-induced RV jitter** at modern echelle resolutions — non-trivial but still subdominant to the predicted K_1.
3. **Single-line spectrum:** at M5.5V, NIR (Y/J/H/K) is preferred over optical. CARMENES, NIRPS, IRD all designed for this regime.
4. **Companion deep in BD territory:** if M_2_true > 13 M_J (which happens if i < 60°), the discovery shifts from "planet" to "BD" — still important but less newsworthy. The BD probability is **~50%** given an isotropic prior over inclinations.
5. **Photocentric vs barycentric ambiguity:** the M_1 = 0.16 M_⊙ Kervella estimate could be biased low by ~10-20% if the M dwarf is metal-poor or the parallax has uncorrected NSS bias. M_1 uncertainty propagates linearly to M_2.
