### Nights to detect K_1 at 5σ

For K_1 ≈ 800 m/s and σ_RV ≈ 10 m/s (HARPS), **3 epochs spanning ~150 days** give >50σ; with FEROS at 20 m/s, **5 epochs** give >40σ.

**One ~3-night HARPS or 5-night FEROS run** is more than enough.

### Risk factors

1. **Faintness:** G = 13.9 is workable but requires 60-min exposures on 2-3m class telescopes. HARPS is comfortable, FEROS needs guide stars.
2. **Eccentricity e = 0.21:** orbital RV curve is asymmetric; periastron passage produces a steeper RV gradient — phase coverage is more important. Should plan epochs to span ~25% of orbital phase around periastron (t_peri at BJD ≈ J2019.4 + 136 d).
3. **Companion at the BD boundary:** ~50% probability the true M_2 is in the BD regime (M_2 > 13 M_J) given isotropic inclinations. This is still a publishable discovery (BD around an M dwarf is rare in itself), but the headline shifts.
4. **Single-line spectrum:** comfortable in the optical for M4-M5V; CARMENES/NIRPS NIR coverage is preferred for late-M precision but optical works.
5. **No prior binary claim in published DR3 NSS papers** (Halbwachs+ 2023, Andrews+ 2022) — this is a clean new detection, NOT a confirmation of a previously-reported binary.
6. **TESS coverage gap at long P:** with only 56 d of actual TESS data in 759 d baseline, no ellipsoidal/transit constraints can be set; M_2 cannot be cross-validated photometrically.
