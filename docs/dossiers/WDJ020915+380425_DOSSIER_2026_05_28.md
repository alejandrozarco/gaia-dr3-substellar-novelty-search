### 2.2 GALEX non-detection — diagnostic interpretation

The GALEX AIS catalog has no detection at this position within 8″. The target lies at b ≈ −22.4°, δ ≈ +38°, in a sky region with GALEX AIS coverage. The predicted single-WD NUV magnitude at T_eff = 14,958 K, R_1 = 0.01149 R_⊙, d = 83.6 pc is **NUV ≈ 15.93 AB** (predicted FUV ≈ 16.89 AB) — well above the AIS nominal depth limit (NUV ~ 20.5 AB, FUV ~ 19.9). The non-detection is therefore unusual and is most plausibly explained by:

1. The target lies in an **AIS sky-coverage gap** in this declination strip (a recurrent issue at high ecliptic-latitude tiles where the GALEX scan pattern was incomplete).
2. The Mast catalog returns no detection because the source falls inside a masked image-edge region.

The MAST GalexView / CASJOBS database (queried separately) confirms no formal detection. A targeted MAST search at deeper MIS/DIS depths could potentially recover a NUV magnitude, but as of the present cone search, **there is no GALEX detection of this target**. This is a single missing band in the SED and is not a critical loss — the remaining 12+ bands of photometry (Pan-STARRS DR2 g/r/i/z/y, Gaia G/BP/RP, AllWISE W1/W2, CatWISE2020 W1/W2) cover the wavelength range where companion-discriminator power resides.

4. **An NS companion is invisible at all wavelengths and indistinguishable from a cool-WD companion using SED alone.**

### 2.5 SED-derived companion class

The SED constrains the companion identity to **one of two classes**:

- **A cool double-degenerate (DD) companion**: an unresolved 1.3-M_⊙ ONe-WD with T_2 ≤ 9,000 K (representative of a CO/ONe WD with cooling age ≳ 1–2 Gyr; this is broadly consistent with the primary's ~250-Myr cooling age inferred from T_1 = 14,958 K, assuming the companion is the older member of the pair).
- **A dormant compact remnant**: a low-mass NS (1.15–1.40 M_⊙) — invisible in the UV/optical/IR because no Roche-lobe overflow or wind accretion is occurring at the 1.05-AU separation.

The discriminator is once again a deep FUV continuum spectrum: HST/COS G130M at SNR ≥ 50 in 1.2 ks would detect a second WD photosphere at T_2 ≥ 25,000 K. Combined with K_1 from the predicted RV campaign, this resolves DD vs WD+NS unambiguously.

### 5.2 Eclipse geometry

With M_total = 2.04 M_⊙, P = 274.5 d, the orbital semi-major axis is a = (0.751² × 2.04)^(1/3) = 1.05 AU. Both stars have R ≈ 0.011 R_⊙ = 5.1 × 10⁻⁵ AU. The geometric eclipse probability is P_eclipse(geo) = (R_1 + R_2)/a ≈ 1.1 × 10⁻⁴, requiring inclination i ≥ 89.993°. **Gaia's NSS reports i = 65.85°** — geometrically excluded from any eclipse. The 0.8° inclination uncertainty does not bring grazing geometry within reach.

### 5.3 P/2 ellipsoidal modulation

Ellipsoidal amplitude A_ell ≈ 0.7 q (R_1/a)³ sin²i = 0.7 × 1.84 × (5.1 × 10⁻⁵)³ × 0.83 ≈ 1.4 × 10⁻¹³ mag — completely undetectable in TESS or any current photometric facility.
