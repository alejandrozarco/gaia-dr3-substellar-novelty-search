**Match diagnostic**:

1. **A hot (T ≥ 20,000 K) WD companion is ruled out at >4σ.** Such a companion would push FUV to ~16 AB (vs the GALEX FUV upper limit of ~20) and the NUV to ~15.8 AB (vs observed 21.1). The NUV alone excludes any companion hotter than ≈ 11,000 K at ≥3σ.
2. **A 1.3-M_⊙ WD companion at T_2 ≲ 9,000 K is fully compatible with all data**: it would shift NUV by ≤ 0.9 mag (within the GALEX 1σ error of 0.23), and the optical bands by ≤ 0.6 mag (within the 2σ envelope on G/BP/RP). The observed NUV of 21.12 ± 0.23 brackets the primary-only prediction (21.07) and the cool-WD-companion prediction (20.22) at the 1σ level.
3. **Any M-dwarf companion is excluded at >10σ.** The M0V scenario would push K to 12.1 (vs the 2MASS limit of ~16.5) and W1 to 12.6 (vs observed 19.99). Even an M5V at T=3,000 K would brighten W1 to 15.52, in flat contradiction with the observed W1 = 19.99 AB.
4. **An NS companion is invisible in GALEX and the IR.** It would produce identical photometry to the primary-only case, fully consistent with all observed data — but it is also formally indistinguishable from a cold-WD companion using this SED alone.

### 2.4 SED-derived companion class

The SED constrains the companion identity to **one of two classes**:

- **A cool double-degenerate (DD) companion**: an unresolved 1.3-M_⊙ ONe-WD with T_2 ≤ 9,000 K (a likely outcome for a CO/ONe WD with cooling age ≳ 1–2 Gyr, broadly consistent with the primary's ~1 Gyr cooling age inferred from T_1 = 7,121 K).
- **A dormant compact remnant**: a low-mass NS (1.27–1.40 M_⊙) — radio-quiet and X-ray-quiet because no Roche-lobe overflow or wind accretion is occurring at the 2.4-AU separation.

The **discriminator** between these two scenarios is a deep FUV continuum spectrum: HST/COS G130M (or G140L) at SNR ≥ 30 in 3 ks would detect a second WD photosphere at T_2 ≥ 25,000 K (i.e. extreme upper limit on companion warmth). Combined with the predicted Hα-RV-derived mass, this resolves DD vs WD+NS unambiguously.

### 5.2 Eclipse geometry

With M_total = 1.98 M_⊙, P = 935 d, the orbital semi-major axis is a = (P²M_total)^(1/3) AU = (2.561² × 1.98)^(1/3) = 2.34 AU. Both stars have R ≈ 0.01 R_⊙ = 4.7 × 10⁻⁵ AU. The geometric eclipse probability is P_eclipse(geo) = (R_1 + R_2)/a ≈ 4 × 10⁻⁵, requiring inclination i ≥ 89.997°. **Gaia's NSS reports i = 66.35°** — geometrically excluded from any eclipse. Even at the 3σ inclination tail (~62–70°), no grazing geometry is reached.

### 5.3 P/2 ellipsoidal modulation

Ellipsoidal amplitude A_ell ≈ 0.7 q (R_1/a)³ × sin²i. With q ≈ 1.36/0.61 = 2.23, R_1/a ≈ 2.5 × 10⁻⁵, sin²i ≈ 0.84 → A_ell ≈ 1.5 × 10⁻¹³ mag. This is **completely undetectable** with any current photometric facility; TESS would need ~10²⁰ s of integration to reach it.

### 5.4 TESS verdict

Both sectors should be re-extracted with `tessbackdrop`/`eleanor` and checked for (i) a 935-d periodicity at any amplitude (no expectation), (ii) any non-orbital variability (flickering or flaring) that would diagnose accretion. The current Gaia DR3 quiescent classification predicts a null on both axes; the search costs ~3 hr of analyst time and forecloses on a contingent positive (e.g. weak X-ray-correlated optical flicker at L_X ≲ 10⁻⁶ × the photospheric luminosity).

### 6.7 Verdict on archival RV

**No archival RV constraint exists for this target.** The complete absence of a spectrum is the dominant gap in the present dossier. The minimal-effort, highest-value next step is a **single X-shooter epoch at any orbital phase** — this would simultaneously confirm the WD spectral class (DA expected), provide the systemic γ-velocity, and break the M_1 atmospheric-model degeneracy via the Hα/Hβ wing fit. ### 6.8 Future Gaia DR4 RV

Gaia's RVS pipeline nominally extends to G_RVS ≈ 14–16 (depending on stellar template). At G = 18.4 the Gaia DR4 RV transit array will not produce a published RV time series for this source. **DR4 will deliver only refined astrometric orbital parameters, not RV.**

## 9. Verdict + RV follow-up plan

### 9.1 Headline classification

**CONFIRMED_WD_BINARY_CHANDRASEKHAR (provisional, pending RV K_1 measurement)**: the multi-wavelength SED is fully accounted for by a single 7,121-K DA WD photosphere; no IR excess at W1/W2 excludes any non-degenerate companion at >5σ; the Gaia DR3 NSS-Orbital f(M) inversion across the full M_1 prior (0.50–0.80 M_⊙) gives M_2 = 1.27–1.52 M_⊙, with the published M_1 placing M_2 just 0.03 M_⊙ below Chandrasekhar (and the +1σ envelope already crossing 1.40 M_⊙).

The dominant outstanding question is **DD (double-degenerate, two WDs) vs WD+NS**. The SED weakly favours DD (no FUV excess from a hot companion, consistent with both a cool WD and a dormant NS), and the population statistics also favour DD by ~50× given the lower NS+WD post-CE channel rate. A FUV continuum measurement (HST/COS, 3 ks) sets the discriminator.

If confirmed as DD via K_1 + γ + FUV, this is **a prime Type Ia progenitor candidate at d = 98 pc** — well within the GW-driven inspiral merger channel for sub-Hubble-time double-degenerate Type Ia explosion at M_total = 1.98 M_⊙ from a current 2.34-AU separation.
