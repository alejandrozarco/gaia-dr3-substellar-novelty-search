## 2. Photometry & SED — companion identity discriminator (KEY SECTION)

A **low-mass He WD** primary (M_WD < 0.5 M_⊙) cannot form by single-star evolution within a Hubble time — the universe is not old enough for a 0.39 M_⊙ He core to cool to a WD via the standard ~10 Gyr horizontal-branch route. The only viable formation channels are:

1. **Stable mass transfer in a binary**: the WD progenitor's hydrogen envelope is stripped by Roche-lobe overflow onto the companion when the progenitor reaches the red-giant branch, leaving a hot bare He core that cools into a He WD.
2. **Common-envelope (CE) ejection**: the companion plunges into the giant's envelope and dynamically ejects it, leaving the bare He core in a much tighter orbit (P_post-CE = hours to days). The 81-d period here is too long for direct post-CE survival — it requires either a wider initial orbit or a non-conservative mass transfer process.

Either way, **M_WD = 0.39 M_⊙ guarantees a binary mass-transfer history**. The Gaia DR3 NSS-Orbital astrometric solution confirms the binary nature directly. The remaining question is: what is the companion?

### 2.3 UV excess interpretation

The dereddened (E(B−V) = 0.06) observed FUV − NUV = 1.94 mag is **bluer than predicted at T = 9,342 K** (predicted intrinsic 2.32). Three explanations are testable:

1. **Slightly hotter primary** (T_eff ≈ 10,000–10,500 K instead of 9,342 K): At T = 10,500 K with R_1 = 0.0180 R_⊙ unchanged, the predicted FUV = 19.55, NUV = 17.45 — matches the observed FUV nearly exactly but overpredicts SDSS g by 0.50 mag. The two extremes (T = 9,342 K matching optical; T = 10,500 K matching UV) bracket a region where a more sophisticated atmosphere model (including H/HeI line blanketing for cool DA He WDs) would resolve the systematic at ~ 10,000 K.

2. **Hot WD companion at very small radius** (T_2 ≈ 25,000–35,000 K, R_2 ≈ 0.005–0.008 R_⊙): Would require a high-mass CO/ONe WD companion at M_2 ≈ 0.9 M_⊙ — but this contradicts the astrometric mass function M_2 ≈ 0.26 M_⊙ at M_1 = 0.39 (unless inclination is severely overestimated). The companion would also dampen photocenter motion at f_2(G) ≈ 0.7, requiring a much larger true a/π and hence even larger M_2. This scenario is energetically and astrometrically inconsistent.

3. **Cool He WD companion** (T_2 ≈ 4,000–6,000 K, R_2 ≈ 0.020–0.025 R_⊙): A LM He WD companion of M_2 ≈ 0.26 M_⊙ formed first in the binary, has been cooling for ≳ 2 Gyr to T_2 < 6,000 K. This companion contributes essentially zero UV flux (cannot explain the UV excess) but also has only a marginal effect on optical (∼ 5% in g) and IR (∼ 3% in W1). This scenario is fully consistent with the SED if we allow a small upward revision of the primary's T_eff to 9,800–10,200 K.

**Adopted interpretation**: A two-He-WD double-degenerate with M_1 = 0.39 M_⊙ (hotter primary, T ≈ 9,500–10,000 K) and M_2 = 0.26 M_⊙ (cooler companion, T ≲ 6,000 K). The companion is essentially invisible at all wavelengths covered here. Definitive discrimination requires medium-resolution spectroscopy to detect the cooler WD's Hα signature in the combined spectrum (if the companion is DA) or radial-velocity follow-up to measure both K_1 and K_2.

### 2.5 SED-derived companion class

The SED constrains the companion identity to a **cool sub-solar-mass WD**, i.e. either:

- **A cool LM He WD**: M_2 ≈ 0.25–0.30 M_⊙, R_2 ≈ 0.020–0.025 R_⊙, T_2 ≤ 6,000 K (cooling age ≳ 2 Gyr).
- **A low-mass CO WD**: M_2 ≈ 0.40–0.50 M_⊙ (if photocenter dampening is significant, raising the true M_2 above the dark-companion estimate), R_2 ≈ 0.013–0.015 R_⊙, T_2 ≤ 6,000 K.

The discriminator is medium-resolution spectroscopy: in either case, a DA companion would show a second set of Hα-Hβ absorption lines (Doppler-shifted at K_2 ≈ 18 km/s relative to the primary's K_1 ≈ 12 km/s — i.e., total Δv ≈ 30 km/s peak-to-peak, easily resolvable at R > 10,000). This is the natural follow-up channel that confirmed the 34 DBL Survey binaries in Munday+ 2024 — but the present source is well below the Munday+ 2024 sample G < 16 cut combined with their specific selection on UV/blue colour.

### 3.2 Why M_1 = 0.39 M_⊙ implies a binary mass-transfer history

The standard single-star route to a He-core WD requires the progenitor to ignite He on the horizontal branch and lose its envelope via wind. For an initial main-sequence mass M_init ≤ 0.95 M_⊙, this requires > 10 Gyr — barely possible. For M_He = 0.39, the progenitor MS lifetime extends to > 14 Gyr — **longer than the age of the universe**. Therefore the only viable channel is **binary mass transfer**: a close companion strips the WD progenitor's envelope before He ignition, leaving a 0.30–0.45 M_⊙ He core that cools as a He WD.

This formation channel is well-established for the **ELM (Extremely Low Mass) WD** population at M < 0.30 M_⊙ (e.g. the Brown ELM Survey series; Pelisoli+ 2018, 2019). The boundary 0.30 ≤ M ≤ 0.45 M_⊙ is the "low-mass He WD" or "shoulder" regime, where formation pathways shift between stable Roche-lobe overflow (RLOF) and Common Envelope (CE) channels:

- **Stable RLOF → wider orbits** (P_orb ~ 10–10,000 d): The donor's envelope is gently transferred to the companion when the donor is on the red giant branch. The remaining He core becomes a WD in a wide detached orbit. For M_WD = 0.39 M_⊙, RLOF predicts P_orb ≈ 50–500 d (Tauris+ 1999, Istrate+ 2014).
- **CE → tight orbits** (P_orb ~ 0.1–10 d): The companion plunges into the donor's envelope and dynamically ejects it. Survives only at P < few d.

**The observed P = 81 d falls cleanly in the stable RLOF predicted regime** for a 0.39 M_⊙ He WD. This is independently consistent with the system's formation as a stable-RLOF descendant — supporting the He WD identification at the level of orbital mechanics.

### 4.1 OrbitalAlternative class — why it matters

The `OrbitalAlternative` NSS class is assigned when the orbital astrometric solution converges but the standard `Orbital` quality cuts are not met — typically because (a) Gaia did not produce a useable RV time series for spectroscopic confirmation, or (b) the source is faint enough that the photocentric signature is at the edge of measurement precision. For **WD-primary systems at G > 16**, OrbitalAlternative is the default: Gaia's RVS pipeline requires G_RVS ≲ 14–16, and bare WD continua at T = 9000 K provide essentially no usable absorption lines for RV cross-correlation. **At G = 16.52, this object falls cleanly below the Gaia DR3 RVS spectroscopic threshold**, and the astrometric-only NSS solution is the strongest available characterization.

### 4.2 NSS parallax cross-check

The NSS-pipeline-reported parallax (π_NSS = 11.075 mas) is offset by +0.38 mas from the single-star gaia_source value (π_GS = 10.696 mas, σ = 0.12). ### 5.2 Eclipse geometry

With M_total = 0.643 M_⊙ and P = 81.45 d:
- Orbital semi-major axis: a = (P²M_total)^(1/3) AU = (0.223² × 0.643)^(1/3) = **0.317 AU = 68.3 R_⊙**
- Both WDs have R ≈ 0.018–0.020 R_⊙
- **Critical inclination for grazing eclipse**: i_crit = arccos[(R_1 + R_2)/a] = **89.97°**
- **Gaia NSS measured inclination**: i = 134.21° (= 45.8° unsigned)
- **Eclipse geometrically EXCLUDED**: i is ~44° away from edge-on

The 1σ uncertainty on the inclination from the Thiele-Innes fit is typically ~ 2–3°. Even at the +3σ tail (i = 39° unsigned), eclipses remain excluded by a wide margin. **No TESS eclipses are expected at any photometric depth.**

### 5.3 Ellipsoidal and Doppler beaming modulation

- **Ellipsoidal amplitude**: A_ell ≈ 0.7 × q × (R_1/a)³ × sin²i = 0.7 × (0.257/0.643) × (0.018×0.0046/0.317)³ × 0.51 = ~ 1 × 10⁻¹³ mag — **utterly undetectable** even in space-based photometry.
- **Doppler beaming**: A_beam ≈ 4 × K_1/c × (1 − dlogF/dlogν) ≈ 4 × (12.2 km/s)/(c) × 1 ≈ 1.6 × 10⁻⁴ mag — at the precision floor of TESS for T_mag = 16, marginally detectable in a stacked 2-sector LC.
- **Light-time effect**: undetectable at this M_total and P.

### 5.4 TESS verdict

Re-extract both sectors with `eleanor` and `tess-bkgd` to obtain background-subtracted light curves. Search for:
1. Eclipses at any phase between sector start/end — geometrically excluded but worth a null check.
2. Doppler beaming at P = 81 d and P/2 = 41 d in the LSP — possible but at ~10⁻⁴ mag, requires careful systematics removal.
3. Non-orbital variability (flickering, magnetic activity, ZZ Ceti-type pulsations at T = 9342 K — yes, this T is at the **cool edge of the ZZ Ceti instability strip!**).

The **ZZ Ceti pulsation possibility** is the most interesting non-orbital signal: at T_eff ≈ 10,000–12,000 K and log g 7.5–7.8, the source sits in the ELM/LM He WD pulsation strip (Hermes+ 2013, Bell+ 2017).
