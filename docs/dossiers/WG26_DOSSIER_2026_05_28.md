### 2.4 Two-component fit: WD + companion

Approximating the WD as a Bergeron-fit DA at T_1 = 21,700 K, R_1 = 0.0132 R_⊙, M_1 = 0.62 M_⊙, and fitting a secondary blackbody whose contribution explains the IR residuals:

Working in fluxes: at W1 the system flux is 10^(1.03/2.5) = 2.59× the predicted WD flux. After subtracting the WD, the *implied secondary* flux ratio is L_2/L_1 (W1) ≈ 1.59 — the companion is about 60% brighter than the WD at W1. This corresponds to a companion with **M_W1 ≈ 12.00 − 2.5*log10(1.59) = 11.50**, i.e. M_W1(companion) ≈ 11.5. Reference values: an M5V has M_W1 ≈ 10.7; an M6V has M_W1 ≈ 11.2; an M7V has M_W1 ≈ 11.7 (Pecaut & Mamajek 2013). **The K-W1 IR excess is consistent with an M6-M7V companion of mass ~ 0.10-0.12 M_⊙.**

**However** this is in direct tension with the orbital mass function (§4) which requires M_2 ≥ 0.55 M_⊙ even at the minimum-M_1 limit. An M6V dwarf (~ 0.10 M_⊙) is excluded at 5+ σ by the astrometric solution. **Reconciliation options** (see also §7 and §9):
  1. The IR excess is not from a single luminous companion but from a *background source* within the 6.1″ AllWISE beam (CatWISE has a much smaller beam and shows smaller excess, +0.15 vs +0.23 in K-W1, lending some weight to a partial blend).
  2. The mass-function value is artificially inflated by the high RUWE (5.0) and AEN_sig (731), i.e. the photocentric ellipse is *not* a clean Keplerian (see §4.3, §7).
  3. The system is a **hierarchical triple**: a tight ~M-dwarf + WD inner pair plus an outer ~M-dwarf making the IR excess, where the astrometric 176-d signal traces the inner orbit.
  4. The Holberg-Bergeron table values I used are off by ~0.3-0.5 mag (i.e. the WD is actually brighter), reducing the IR residual significantly. **A proper Koester/Tremblay model-atmosphere SED fit at the published T_eff, log g is required** to confirm/reject the apparent excess.

### 2.5 GALEX non-detection

No FUV or NUV detection at this position in GALEX AIS or any nearby survey field. The GALEX AIS depth is ~20.5 (AB) in FUV and ~21.0 in NUV. A 22,000-K DA WD at 56 pc should reach AB(FUV) ≈ 14.0 and AB(NUV) ≈ 14.2 — bright enough to *trivially* be detected if GALEX had a tile at this position. The GALEX_AIS footprint excludes ~10% of the sky for various reasons (bright-source masking, scheduling gaps). The absence here is consistent with a missed-tile rather than a real non-detection of UV flux. **The GALEX null is uninformative.** A targeted GALEX-archive query of the deeper MIS and DIS surveys returns no detection either, suggesting the field was simply not observed by GALEX during the mission lifetime.

### 2.6 SkyMapper u−v "Balmer-line break" diagnostic

SkyMapper has 6 bands including the u (3500 Å) and v (3850 Å) bands that bracket the Balmer jump at 3646 Å. Observed u − v = −0.137 (AB). For a hot DA WD with deep Balmer absorption and a flat photospheric continuum near the Balmer jump, this colour is close to the expected ~−0.10 to −0.15 (slight Paschen continuum decrement, modest H Balmer lines). **No evidence of an obvious second-source UV-bright contributor** (which would push u − v positive or strongly positive depending on T_2). Consistent with a single hot DA WD primary.

**Adopted primary parameters (Sahu 2023 HST/COS, the gold-standard UV-spectroscopic fit)**:
- T_eff = **21,700 K** (cooling age = 42 Myr — quite young)
- log g = 7.99 (essentially the canonical CO-WD value)
- M_1 = **0.62 ± 0.02 M_⊙** (a typical CO WD; sits on the M-Z relation for ~ 2.5-3.0 M_⊙ MS progenitor)
- R_1 ≈ 0.0132 R_⊙ (from R = √(GM/g))
- L_bol ≈ 0.035 L_⊙ → M_bol ≈ +8.4
- Cooling age 42-44 Myr — **the youngest WD in the dossier set**; the system was a hot, recently-emerged DA WD when Gaia DR3 observed it.

The mass spread M_1 = 0.43 → 0.62 across the four fits is significant. The Shahaf HST/COS UV spectroscopic value is the most reliable single measurement because (i) the UV resonance lines (Lyα, Lyβ) are far more sensitive to log g than the Balmer lines, (ii) the photometric fits are degenerate between T_eff and log g due to the cool-end CO-WD cooling-track cliff. The GF21 photometric value (M_1 = 0.528) is a useful lower-bracket but should not be preferred over HST/COS.

From the Gaia DR3 Ca H&K synthetic-photometry catalog (V/167), F(Ca H&K) = 1.39e-16 erg/s/cm²/Å, CaHK synthetic magnitude = 14.30 (AB), **Pvar = 0.874**. **The Pvar = 0.874 indicates a 87% probability that the source is photometrically variable in Gaia BP/RP** — supporting the binary-induced photocentric/photometric variability hypothesis (e.g. due to ellipsoidal modulation, reflection, eclipses, or pulsation). However for a P = 176 d orbit, the variability amplitude would be modest (~ 1% from ellipsoidal modulation; ~ 0.01% from Doppler beaming).

### 3.3 Cooling age and progenitor
Sahu 2023 give cooling age = 42 Myr (Montreal grid) / 44 Myr (Levenhagen-Pereira grid). The young cooling age is consistent with M_1 ≈ 0.62 M_⊙ and T_1 ≈ 22 kK (Bedard+ 2020). The MS progenitor mass via the IFMR is M_init ≈ 2.5–3.0 M_⊙ (Cummings 2018), with a MS lifetime of ~ 500 Myr — meaning the system's total age is ~ 0.5 Gyr.

### 4.4 Caveats: RUWE = 5.0, AEN_sig = 731

The Gaia DR3 single-star astrometric solution carries **RUWE = 5.012** and **astrometric_excess_noise_sig = 731**, both extreme outliers compared to a clean isolated source (typical RUWE < 1.2, AEN_sig < 10). At G = 14.35 these values are diagnostic of:

1. **Unresolved binary photocentric motion**: the dominant explanation, since the NSS pipeline successfully fits an Orbital solution with significance 68.76. The 1.87-mas parallax shift between single-star and NSS fits (= 0.34 a_phot) is exactly the magnitude expected for absorbed orbital wobble.
2. **Resolvable nearby blend at 0.1″–0.5″**: not consistent with the IPD diagnostic (ipd_frac_multi_peak = 0; ipd_gof_harmonic_amplitude = 0.063 — modest but not extreme). This is therefore *unlikely* to be the dominant cause.
3. **Hierarchical triple**: an inner 176-d binary plus an outer wider companion at P > 1000 d. The IPD diagnostic is still consistent (the outer would be at separation ≪ Gaia's 100 mas resolution if M_3 ≈ 0.5 M_⊙ at d = 56 pc), and the NSS Orbital pipeline would fit only the short-period inner motion. **This is testable** via Gaia DR4 acceleration solutions and via radial-velocity monitoring sensitive to long-term γ-drift.

In short, the orbit *itself* is reliable (significance 68.76; the photocentric ellipse is well-detected and well-sampled at 371 AL observations), but the high RUWE warns us that either the orbital amplitude is partly diluted by a luminous companion (reducing the true a_phot), or that there is an additional long-period acceleration not captured by the Keplerian-only model. **Both scenarios push the inferred M_2 *up*, not down**, so 0.65 M_⊙ is a soft lower bound at the adopted M_1.

### 4.5 Implied K_1 (for RV follow-up)

Assuming a randomly-oriented orbit, the expected projected radial-velocity semi-amplitude of the WD primary is:

K_1 = (2πG/(P))^(1/3) × M_2 sin(i) / (M_1 + M_2)^(2/3) / √(1-e²)

At M_1 = 0.62, M_2 = 0.65 (dark), P = 175.94 d, e = 0.064:
- sin(i) = 1: K_1 ≈ **21.1 km/s**
- sin(i) = 0.87 (50th percentile of random orientation): K_1 ≈ 18.4 km/s
- sin(i) = 0.5: K_1 ≈ 10.6 km/s

The expected K_1 is large enough that **even a single archival RV epoch at 1 km/s precision (easily attainable on Hα of a G = 14.35 WD with X-shooter or UVES) would already provide a > 10σ test of binary motion** at the most-probable inclination, and 5–10 epochs would solve K_1 to ±0.5 km/s.

### 5.3 Ellipsoidal modulation, Doppler beaming

At a_rel = 143 R_⊙, R_1/a = 9 × 10⁻⁵; ellipsoidal amplitude on the WD due to a 0.65 M_⊙ companion is ≈ 0.7 q (R_1/a)³ × sin²i ≈ 6 × 10⁻¹³ mag — utterly undetectable.

On an M-dwarf companion at R_2 = 0.6 R_⊙, A_ell ≈ 5 × 10⁻⁸ mag — still undetectable, because the M-dwarf contributes ≪ 1% of the system flux in the TESS band (the WD dominates by ~1.5-2 mag in TESS's broad red optical bandpass).

Doppler beaming amplitude: A_beam ≈ 4 × K_1 / c ≈ 4 × 21.1/3e5 ≈ **2.8 × 10⁻⁴ mag** at sin i = 1. At T = 14.4, TESS achieves ~0.5–1 mmag per-point precision per 120 s; folded over ~6 orbital cycles spanning the 5 sectors, the Doppler-beaming amplitude is **at or below the TESS noise floor** — not a practical detection target unless multiple sectors are exquisitely cleaned.

### 7.7 Hierarchical triple test

To test whether WG 26 is a hierarchical triple (inner 176-d binary + outer companion at > 10 yr period):

- **Gaia DR3 alone cannot rule this out** — the 34-month DR3 baseline samples > 7 inner orbital periods but only a small fraction of an outer P_3 > 10 yr.
- **The IPD diagnostics are weakly consistent** with no outer companion (no multi-peak), but the harmonic-amplitude 0.063 hints at *some* extra astrometric structure beyond a pure Keplerian.
- **Gaia DR4 (2027) and DR5 (2030) astrometry will resolve this** with longer baseline. A residual after subtracting the NSS Orbital model will appear as long-period acceleration or extra astrometric noise.
- **The SED-IR-excess at K-W1 of 0.15-0.23 (§2)** would, if real, be consistent with an unresolved outer M-dwarf at ≲ 1″ — i.e. exactly the configuration where Gaia would not resolve it but it would contribute strongly to AllWISE/CatWISE photometry.

### 9.3 The IR-excess vs. mass-function tension

The K-W1 mild IR excess (+0.15-0.23 mag) seen in §2 is *qualitatively* consistent with an M6-M7V dwarf (~ 0.10 M_⊙), but *quantitatively inconsistent* with the astrometric mass function (which demands M_2 ≳ 0.55 M_⊙ at any plausible M_1). **The tension can only be resolved by**:
1. High-resolution imaging (HST or 8-m AO at K-band) — to confirm whether the IR excess is truly co-located with the WD primary or is an AllWISE-only blend
2. Single-epoch X-shooter spectrum — to rule out an M-dwarf companion via the chromospheric CaII H&K emission lines (an M-dwarf at any sub-solar mass produces detectable Hα and CaII chromospheric flux, which would be seen as residuals after WD-photosphere subtraction)
3. JWST MIRI at 5-15 μm — to characterise the IR excess shape and determine T_eff, R of any companion at 0.1″ separation
