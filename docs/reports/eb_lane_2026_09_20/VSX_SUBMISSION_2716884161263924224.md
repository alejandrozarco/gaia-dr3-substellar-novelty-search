## 2.1 Eclipse depth was under-reported, and the eclipse is GREY

The depth of a V-shaped eclipse depends entirely on how close to the vertex you sample. Measured
directly from the epochs, with no eclipse model:

| sampling window | zr | zg | zi |
|---|---|---|---|
| +/-0.008 phase (+/-23 min) | 0.669 | 0.674 | 0.647 |
| +/-0.015 phase (+/-43 min) | 0.554 | 0.618 | 0.571 |
| **faintest observed epoch** | **0.778** | **0.778** | **0.772** |

The REVISION 1 value of 0.686 mag (zr) was a trapezoid-fit depth parameter averaged across the V,
not the depth at minimum. **Mid-eclipse depth is ~0.78 mag**, and max/min in §2 are updated to the
observed extremes, which is what VSX wants.

**The eclipse is achromatic: 0.778 / 0.778 / 0.772 mag in g / r / i, equal to within 0.006 mag**
across three independent bands. A phase-binned colour analysis gives out-of-eclipse
(g-r) = 0.4985 +/- 0.0013 and Delta(g-r) = +0.0236 +/- 0.0129 in eclipse - marginally redder at
1.8 sigma, i.e. consistent with grey.

**This bears directly on the period ambiguity of §6.** A grey eclipse means the eclipsed surface
has essentially the same colour as the system as a whole, i.e. the two components are near-twins in
temperature. Near-twins produce two near-identical eclipses per orbit - which is exactly the
configuration under which the adopted period is HALF the orbital period. Combined with §6 (alternate
minima equal to within 0.08 mag), the balance of evidence now favours the orbital period being
**2 x 1.984637 = 3.969274 d**, with the adopted value being the photometric (single-eclipse) period.
The submission should adopt the shorter period by convention but say this explicitly in the remarks.

## 2.2 Period: a hint of a correction that does NOT survive scrutiny

An O-C analysis using season-stacked minima (no single eclipse is sampled well enough to time
individually - 3 to 6 in-eclipse points per night at best) gives 14 usable minima across
1359 cycles and 3 bands. A weighted linear fit gives a period correction of
**+0.206 +/- 0.057 s/cycle (3.6 sigma)** -> P = 1.9846373 +/- 0.0000007 d.

**But chi2/dof = 2.68**, so the per-season timing errors are underestimated; inflating by
sqrt(2.68) reduces the trend to **2.2 sigma**. A second, independent implementation of the same
analysis returned +0.29 +/- 0.06 s/cycle and P = 1.9846389 - differing from the first by ~1.5 sigma.
**Two reasonable implementations disagree, so the correction is not robust and is not adopted as
fact.**

What IS solid is the value itself: our O-C gives 1.9846373 and **ATLAS independently reports
fp-period = 1.984637**. Independent agreement between a photographic-era-independent survey and our
O-C is worth more than either fit alone. Hence the adopted **P = 1.984637 +/- 0.000005 d**, an
uncertainty chosen to span the full range of methods tried (1.9846349 to 1.9846449).

No period change: the quadratic O-C term is 0.2 sigma. |Pdot| < 0.23 s/yr (3 sigma).

## 2.3 The blend hypothesis is now closed, on imaging

Pan-STARRS1 stack analysis of the r-band FITS cutout (0.25"/pix):

- **FWHM 1.07"** from the radial profile - normal PS1 stack seeing, not extended or double
- **ellipticity |e| = 0.011** (a marginally-resolved blend would show ~0.1)
- radial profile falls monotonically, no shoulder or plateau; not saturated
- **PSF minus Kron = -0.059 (r), -0.061 (i)** - a point source, not a galaxy
- **QfPerfect = 0.9994 / 0.9994 / 0.9998 (g/r/i)** - PSF flux falls on clean unmasked pixels
- **nothing at all within 17.8"**; exactly 5 sources above 8 sigma in the whole 60" field
- PS1 DR2 catalogue position lies 0.015" from the Gaia position

Independent photometric confirmation of the baseline: PS1 gMeanPSFMag = 16.8161 +/- 0.0057 against
our ZTF zg out-of-eclipse 16.816 - **agreement to 0.000 mag**; rMeanPSFMag 16.3700 vs our 16.318.
PS1's own 9 r-epochs and 13 g-epochs show spans of only 0.035 and 0.067 mag, i.e. PS1 never caught
an eclipse - unremarkable at 9.1% duty (P ~ 0.42 and ~0.29 respectively).

## 2.4 No mid-infrared excess

WISE W3 (12 um) and W4 (22 um) are non-detections; the source is detected in the unWISE NEO7 deep
coadds at W1/W2 and marginally in 2MASS J/H/Ks. **No warm dust**, which disfavours a
disk-occultation / YSO "dipper" interpretation and supports an ordinary stellar eclipse.

## 2.5 Persistent and stellar across a 72-year imaging baseline

Detected and stellar on POSS-I E (1954-07-30), POSS-II F (1990-08-20), POSS-II J (1991-08-18),
POSS-II N (1995-07-21) and the PS1 stack (2012-02-05). No historic outburst, dropout or
displacement - a normal persistent star, as an eclipsing binary should be.

# REVISION 4 (2026-09-20) — TESS FFI extracted; period independently confirmed, degeneracy NOT broken

![TESS odd/even](tess_oddeven.png)

The review named TESS FFI photometry as the highest-value next check, on the grounds that a
within-sector odd/even test would "attack C4, which holds essentially all the remaining
probability mass." The data were extracted. **That reasoning was wrong, and the reason is
structural** — but the extraction delivered something more valuable instead.

## 4.1 What TESS confirms

Sectors **56** (2022) and **83** (2024), camera 1 CCD 3, 158 s FFI cadence, TIC 415929979
(Tmag 15.907, **Teff 5777 K** — solar, independently consistent with the twin-G2V reading).
20,803 good cadences.

- **Blind BLS, with no ephemeris assumed, recovers P = 1.984660 d** against the ZTF value
  1.9846349 — agreement to 1.3 × 10⁻⁵, from a different instrument, a different bandpass
  (600–1000 nm vs ZTF r) and epochs 4–6 years later. Depth 41.5%, SNR 13.3.
- **The ZTF ephemeris predicts the eclipses correctly years later**: folding TESS on it gives
  a depth of 55.9% ± 0.5% at phase 0 across 24 eclipses. The HJD→BTJD transfer closes.

This is the strongest independent confirmation the object has, and it raises **C1** rather than C4.

## 4.2 The odd/even test — and why it cannot settle the period

Within-sector (immune to inter-sector systematics, as the review specified):

| sector | odd | even | relative difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| 56 | 49.79 ± 1.64 % | 51.86 ± 0.86 % | −4.07 ± 3.65 % (1.12σ) |
| 83 | 61.35 ± 0.81 % | 61.25 ± 0.89 % | +0.17 ± 1.96 % (0.09σ) |
| **combined** | | | **−0.78 ± 1.73 % = 0.45σ** |

**3σ limit on any relative depth difference: 5.2%**, equivalent to 0.028 mag. The two sectors
agree with each other at 1.02σ. This is roughly twice as tight as the ZTF limit and is the best
constraint available.

**But it does not discriminate between the two period hypotheses, and no improvement in
precision ever could.** A null odd/even result is what *both* hypotheses predict:

- If P_orb = 1.9846349 d with one eclipse per orbit, then "odd" and "even" at the doubled period
  are **the same physical eclipse**. Their equality is guaranteed by construction — it is not a
  measurement.
- If P_orb = 3.9692698 d with twin components, equality means the two stars are very similar.

The test can only ever *refute* the twin hypothesis by finding a difference. Finding none is
uninformative between them. The review's claim that this check attacks C4 was mistaken.

## 4.3 TESS cannot test the 50% twin ceiling either

The physical argument in REVISION 3 cites "the 48.5% depth sitting just under the 50.0% ceiling
that identical twins impose". **TESS's absolute depth is not reliable enough to test that**: the
same eclipse measures **51.28% in sector 56 and 60.93% in sector 83**, a 9.6-point spread driven
by per-sector background subtraction in a field where the target contributes only ~5% of the
aperture flux (32 e/s of target against ~128 e/s/px of sky). The *differential* odd/even
measurement is robust because both minima are measured identically within a sector; the absolute
depth is not. **ZTF PSF photometry remains the authority on eclipse depth.**

## 4.4 Consequence

**C4 is unchanged at ~0.08.** The discriminator remains the physical argument of REVISION 3
(a 48% drop requires R₂ ≥ 0.67 R₁, while no secondary eclipse at phase 0.5 of the half period
caps T₂ < 3000 K, which no equilibrium star satisfies at R ≈ 0.9 R☉). TESS neither strengthens
nor weakens it. **C1 rises to ≈0.995** on the independent blind recovery.

What would actually settle C4 is **spectroscopy**: two sets of lines moving in antiphase with a
3.969-d period, or a single-lined 1.985-d solution. Radial velocities, not more photometry.

## 4.5 A processing error caught and recorded

The first extraction returned a flat null — blind BLS SNR 0.0, depth 0.08% where ~5% was expected.
The cause was in our own code, not the data: `create_threshold_mask(threshold=10,
reference_pixel="center")` could not select the target, which sits only ~4σ above sky
(143 e/s against 125 e/s), and the aperture latched onto unrelated flux. The tell was arithmetic
and was visible in the first printout: **694 e/s of "target" flux where Tmag 15.907 predicts ~65.**
Re-extracting with an aperture pinned to the target's WCS pixel position gave 32 e/s, and the
eclipse appeared immediately at 56%. *A null from a faint-source aperture must be checked against
the flux the catalogue magnitude predicts before it is believed.*
