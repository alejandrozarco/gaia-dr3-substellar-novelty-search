## 1. Outburst characterization -- the claim does not survive quality cuts

Raw ATLAS forced photometry (`/tmp/atlas_batch/lc_5808732887468490368.txt`, sorted,
n=2079: 478 c-band, 1601 o-band, MJD 59601-61221, ~1620 d baseline) shows 7 c-band and
22 o-band epochs flagged bright by a naive sigma/amplitude cut (>5sigma, uJy>150),
matching the prior screen's `n_outburst_events_c=7, n_outburst_events_o=22`.

**But every one of these "bright" epochs has a catastrophically bad PSF fit.** ATLAS's
own per-epoch reduced chi-squared (`chi/N`, the goodness of the single-PSF forced-photometry
fit) rises monotonically and steeply with flux excursion size:

| |uJy| bin | median chi/N | n |
|---|---:|---:|
| -100 to 100 (baseline) | 13.7 | 1491 |
| 100-500 | 48.1 | 154 |
| 500-1000 | 468.6 | 60 |
| >1000 | 831.8 | 36 |

Spearman correlation of signed uJy vs chi/N: r=0.29, p=3.5e-42 (n=2079). A real point-source
outburst should fit the PSF model just as well brightened as quiescent (chi/N ~ 1-5
throughout); instead the fit quality collapses exactly when flux is highest -- the classic
signature of a second, unmodeled light source (crowding/blend) breaking the single-PSF
assumption, not genuine brightening. Several of the largest "outbursts" also show inflated
PSF shape parameters (e.g. MJD 59821.86: maj=5.78 arcsec, min=5.40 arcsec vs the typical
2.2-2.9/1.8-2.5 arcsec for this field), independently confirming shape distortion from an
unresolved second source.

**Quality-cut + nightly-binned test (the decisive check).** Excluding chi/N>50 epochs and
computing inverse-variance-weighted nightly means per band (`nightly_binned_goodfit.csv`,
443 nightly bins) leaves a **flat, quiescent light curve indistinguishable from noise** in
both bands (see `outburst_before_after.png`, bottom panel). The 20 most significant surviving
nightly excursions are ALL negative (fainter than the static reference template used for ATLAS
difference imaging) -- there is no positive/brightening signal left once bad-fit epochs are
removed. The single largest raw excursion (MJD 61193.01, uJy=19340) has an error of 16984
(S/N~1.1, not significant) and chi/N=1.09 -- an isolated single-frame fluctuation/near-noise
point, not part of any multi-epoch brightened state.

**No true multi-night persistence.** Of the 97 gaps between flagged nights, only 9/97 are
1-day apart; there is no run of consecutive brightened nights. Real dwarf-nova outbursts
persist for days to weeks (recognizable as a plateau in nightly-binned data); nothing like
that appears here.

**c/o "coincidence" is not statistically meaningful.** Only 1 night in the whole baseline
has both c- and o-band data (ATLAS's c/o cadence barely overlaps for this field), so the
prior screen's `band_consistent_outburst=True` flag must have used a loose (~days-scale)
window. With both bands independently flagged at a base rate of ~7-8% of all epochs (35/478 c,
125/1601 o) -- itself already suspicious for real astrophysical variability, and explained by
the same PSF-fit-degradation mechanism acting on both filters -- a random 4-day window has a
~27% Poisson chance of containing a flagged o-point. The observed "coincidences" are fully
consistent with the shared systematic (crowding) leaking into both bands together, not
independent chromatic confirmation of one real event.

**Conclusion of step 1:** no genuine outburst episodes exist in this light curve at any
amplitude, duration, or recurrence worth calling a dwarf nova. What looked like "7 cyan + 22
orange outbursts" is a data-quality artifact.

`outburst_before_after.png` (raw vs. quality-cut nightly-binned, side by side).

The target's own Gaia astrometric solution has **RUWE = 23.98** -- an extreme outlier
(flag threshold is 1.4) -- meaning even Gaia's own 5-parameter single-star fit is badly
broken at this position, independently corroborating a crowded/blended field before ATLAS
is even considered. This is most simply explained by contamination from the 6.85 arcsec,
1.24-mag-brighter neighbour perturbing Gaia's own image-parameter determination.

**eRASS1 X-ray position vs both stars:** the eRASS1 catalog entry (VizieR J/A+A/682/A34,
Merloni+2024) gives X-ray position RA=248.619693, Dec=-69.563427. Separations:
- to target: **9.26 arcsec**
- to bright neighbour: **5.24 arcsec**

The X-ray centroid sits closer to the bright neighbour than to the nominal target -- but
this X-ray detection is itself extremely marginal (net counts MLcts1 = 5.64 +/- 2.6-2.9,
S/N ~ 2.6 sigma, background 1.27 cts, exposure only 165 s), so eROSITA's positional
uncertainty at this significance is comparable to or larger than the 4 arcsec gap between
the two candidate hosts -- this is a soft preference for the neighbour, not a clean
discriminant on its own.

**SkyMapper DR4/DR1.1** independently resolves both stars at their catalog positions
with photometry matching the Gaia magnitudes (target: g=16.06, r=15.65, i=15.41;
neighbour: g=15.09, r=14.53, i=14.13) -- both quiescent, no epoch caught either star
brightened, consistent with (not able to independently confirm or refute beyond) the
ATLAS finding of no real outburst.

**Given there is no real outburst (step 1), the localization question is moot for a DN
claim** -- but for completeness: even if one entertained the outburst as real, the
1.24-mag-brighter neighbour with a clean RUWE, an X-ray position 1.8x closer to it, and a
Gaia `VARIABLE` flag would be the far more plausible host than the RUWE=24 target.

## 3. Novelty (multi-catalogue)

- **SIMBAD:** null at both target and neighbour positions (20 arcsec cone) and by direct
  Gaia-DR3-ID lookup for the neighbour. "No astronomical object found" / "Identifier
  not found."
- **VSX:** no entry for the target. The bright neighbour **is** listed (VSX OID 5944929,
  "Gaia DR3 5808732853108753536", 16:34:28.67-69:33:53.5, TrA, **Type=RS**, Gmag range
  14.48-14.63, spectral type K) -- this is an automatic re-publication of the same Gaia
  DR3 `vari_classifier_result`/`vari_short_timescale` data we already have, not an
  independent discovery. The listed "period" (0.0147176 d = 21.2 min) is explicitly
  sourced from Gaia's short-timescale table (no dedicated period search), is physically
  implausible for an RS CVn, and is not independent corroboration of anything.
- **No CV/DN catalogue match anywhere:** no VSX UG/UGSU/UGSS/UGZ tag, no Ritter-Kolb
  (B/cb), no Downes (V/123A), absent from Rodriguez+2025 (eRASS1xGaia published CV
  selection), from Liu+2025 (arXiv:2505.10478, 22 eRASS1 accreting-binary candidates),
  from arXiv:2606.01085 (43 periodic + 1958-source compact-binary sample), and from
  arXiv:2504.10794 (eROSITA-DE/XMM/Swift/ROSAT CV candidates). This project's local
  `known_objects` store independently returns `known=False` for both target and
  neighbour positions.
- **Literature/web:** "eRASS1 J163428.7-693348" / "1eRASS J163428.7-693348" and the bare
  coordinates return zero hits anywhere indexed (arXiv, ADS-linked pages).
- **Gaia DR3 variability:** target has `phot_variable_flag=NOT_AVAILABLE`,
  `non_single_star=0` -- no variability or binarity flag of any kind from Gaia itself.
  Neighbour: `in_vari_classification_result=True`, best_class="RS" (score 0.67),
  `in_vari_short_timescale=True`, but peak-to-peak amplitude over 994 days is only
  ~0.15 mag (G), ~0.16 (BP), ~0.15 (RP) -- small-amplitude spot modulation, not
  multi-magnitude eruptive behavior.

**Result: this is a genuinely uncatalogued field (both components) -- but "uncatalogued"
here means "nothing unusual has ever needed cataloguing," not "novel discovery." The
neighbour's own classification (RS CVn) is corroborated, not contradicted, by every
external source; there is no CV/DN hiding in this field under any name.**

## 4. X-ray accretion consistency

eRASS1 (J/A+A/682/A34) full-band (0.2-2.3 keV) flux: MLFlux1 = 3.18e-14 erg/s/cm2,
from a marginal 5.64-count (2.6 sigma) detection, 165 s exposure.

Using the target's own (RUWE-corrupted, but only external distance estimate available)
parallax of 6.07 mas -> d ~ 165 pc:

- Lx ~ 1.03e29 erg/s
- Lx/Lbol ~ 1-5e-4 (for a K-type dwarf with Lbol ~ 0.05-0.3 Lsun), i.e. **at or below**
  the ~1e-3 coronal-saturation line, not above it.

This X-ray luminosity is also **10-100x fainter than typical RS CVn coronae** (~1e30-1e31
erg/s), so it sits comfortably in the ordinary coronal-activity regime for either a
modestly active dwarf or a lower-activity RS CVn component -- there is no excess demanding
an accretion origin, independent of the outburst-photometry finding.

## Bottom line

**NOT_DN.** The headline "chromatic-consistent outburst" that made this the lane's
strongest lead does not survive a basic data-quality check: every flagged bright epoch in
both ATLAS bands has a catastrophically degraded PSF fit (chi/N up to ~20,000, rising
monotonically with flux excursion), and the light curve is flat/quiescent once bad-fit
epochs are excluded and nights are properly binned. This is an artifact of crowded-field
forced photometry (corroborated independently by the target's own extreme Gaia RUWE=23.98),
not a real astrophysical brightening on the target, the bright RS CVn neighbour, or any
other star in the field. The X-ray flux is modest and consistent with ordinary coronal
emission (Lx/Lbol at/below the saturation line), so there is also no accretion-luminosity
case independent of the photometry. The field (target + neighbour) is genuinely
uncatalogued/unclassified in the literature, but nothing in it is a hidden known CV either
-- it is simply an unremarkable, previously-unstudied faint coronal X-ray source plus its
brighter RS CVn neighbour.

**Recommendation:** log as a `findings_register.csv` row (uncatalogued eRASS1 source,
disposition: outburst claim retracted after quality-cut re-analysis, classification
`coronal_or_quiet` / blend-explained-artifact), not a full journal. No human review needed
for a discovery claim -- there isn't one. A short human-facing note may be worth adding to
`docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md` / the discovery-menu lane write-up flagging that the ATLAS-bridge
outburst screen's `chi/N` (PSF-fit quality) column should be used as a hard pre-filter in
any future re-run of this lane on the other 14 bridge objects -- this single false lead
consumed the deep-dive slot and the same chi/N-vs-flux artifact likely explains some of the
other "candidate-artifact-outburst" tier hits too (worth a cheap batch re-check).
