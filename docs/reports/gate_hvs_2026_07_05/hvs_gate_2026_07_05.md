# Gate check: DESI DR1 x Gaia extreme-velocity / HVS pivot — 2026-07-05

## Verdict: NO_GO (as a "novel discovery" lane) — the exact search this gate
tested is already published, twice, in 2025-2026, by groups actively working
DESI DR1 + Gaia for exactly this purpose.

## 1. Access + technical funnel (real data, reused from today's dark-companion hunt)

(`/tmp/desi_hunt/checkpoints/rv_stats_merged.parquet`, 1,211,271 unique
multi-epoch physical stars, built earlier today for a different lane —
per-star weighted-mean heliocentric RV `wmean_rv` from individual epoch VRAD,
no Galactocentric transform applied at this stage, per the gate brief's
"extreme heliocentric RV first" framing).

Within the 379: >450 km/s = 125, >500 = 19, >550 = 3, **none exceed ~565 km/s**
(the pipeline's own `rv_implausible` flag at |RV|>600 fires on zero of these —
consistent with genuine stellar velocities, not catastrophic-fit artifacts).

**This is a real, quality-screened, multi-epoch-robust pool — the technical
funnel does NOT kill the lane.** The `drv_max_sigma` diagnostic (max pairwise
epoch-to-epoch RV difference, in combined-error sigma) sits at 0.1-3.3 for the
top 10 — i.e., all epochs *agree* with each other at the extreme value, which
is the correct signature of a genuinely extreme systemic RV rather than one
bad exposure driving a spurious mean (contrast with the sibling dark-companion
lane today, where the RV-variability tail was dominated by catastrophic single-
epoch fit failures).

## 2. Confirmability spot-check (the method-subtlety the brief flagged)

Pulled Gaia DR3 astrometry for the top 15 by |v_helio|. Result confirms the
brief's warned caveat directly:
- **0/15 have a Gaia DR3 radial_velocity** (all NaN) — these stars are below
  the Gaia RVS trigger threshold; DESI is the only RV source. No independent
  RV cross-check is possible from Gaia itself for this exact population — this
  IS the DESI-adds-value case, not a red flag.
- **Parallax S/N is low for 14/15** (parallax_over_error mostly 0.5-4, one
  outlier at 11.3) — exactly the "distance is the weak link" problem the brief
  anticipated.
- For the one star with a usable parallax (source_id 2744139439608762112,
  plx S/N=11.3, d≈1.43 kpc [1.31-1.57 kpc at ±1σ]): computed full 3D
  Galactocentric velocity = **420 km/s** (stable across the parallax
  uncertainty range, 420-420 km/s) — **below** the local Galactic escape
  velocity (~500-550 km/s). A heliocentric RV of -550 km/s, once projected into
  the Galactocentric frame with real proper motion and distance, is NOT
  actually unbound for this star. This is the base-rate outcome the brief
  warned about: most large heliocentric RVs are large-but-bound halo/disk
  kinematics, not HVS ejecta, once geometry is included.
- Net: confirmability is real (a systemic RV is a direct measurement, no
  telescope needed) but for the *majority* of the 379, distance is too poor to
  resolve bound-vs-unbound; only the extreme heliocentric RV itself is a robust,
  archive-only, distance-independent statement — which is exactly the framing
  the brief specified as the "clean" search. That framing holds up technically.

## 3. Prior-art / scoop finding (DECISIVE — this is what kills the lane)

Two systematic DESI DR1 + Gaia DR3 extreme-velocity/HVS searches are already
published, by groups with direct access to DESI collaboration data products
and Koposov (DESI MWS catalog's own lead author) directly involved in one:

- **Verberne et al. 2025**, "Searching for stars ejected from the Galactic
  Centre in DESI," arXiv:2506.19570 (June 2025). Statistical/ensemble search
  for a kinematic overdensity of low-angular-momentum, high-metallicity DESI
  stars consistent with Galactic Centre ejecta. **No detection**; sets an
  ejection-rate upper limit (~2.8e-3/yr) and an upper limit of ~51 possible
  GC-ejecta stars hiding in DESI. No individual candidate list published.
- **Cavieres, Koposov, Rossi, Penoyre & Verberne 2026**, "Discovery of
  Galactic center ejected star in DESI DR1," arXiv:2601.19866, published A&A
  709, A117 (May 2026). Explicit systematic 6D search (DESI spectroscopy +
  Gaia astrometry) across DESI DR1. Found **one** star, **DESI-312**
  (G≈17.1 — squarely inside our G=16-18.5 target band), ejection velocity
  698 km/s, [Fe/H]=+0.27, traced back to within 2 kpc of the Galactic Centre.
  This paper explicitly frames DESI+Gaia as extending Marchetti-style
  Gaia-only HVS searches to fainter magnitudes — i.e., it has **already
  claimed the exact "faint DESI HVS" niche** this gate set out to test.
- **Deng et al. 2026**, "An Old, Low-mass, Metal-poor Hypervelocity Star
  Candidate Consistent with a Galactic Center Origin," arXiv:2604.21646
  (ApJL, April 2026) — an independent group/method, same DESI DR1 + Gaia DR3
  combination, different star (**DESI-HVS1**, v=523 km/s ejection velocity,
  0.8 M⊙, [Fe/H]=-1.6, age~14 Gyr). Confirms this is now a **multi-group,
  actively-contested niche**, not a single-object note.
- Context (established prior HVS catalogs, for magnitude-regime comparison):
  Marchetti+2021 (arXiv:2012.02123, Gaia EDR3 6D, G≲13, 94 candidates >50%
  unbound), Marchetti+2022 (arXiv:2206.06962, Gaia DR3 6D, 34M stars, 0 robust
  candidates above 700 km/s), Li/LAMOST systematic search (arXiv:2504.14836,
  LAMOST DR10, 125 candidates v>300 km/s), Brown et al. SDSS/MMT series
  (targeted blue-star survey, not magnitude-complete).
- The DESI MWS stellar-catalogue paper itself (Koposov+2026, arXiv:2505.14787)
  does not flag extreme-RV stars in its abstract, but its lead author is a
  coauthor on the Cavieres HVS discovery — the same team pivoted directly from
  cataloguing to HVS-hunting.

**This is not a single-object note (like the Smith 2026 RNAAS note in
today's sibling dark-companion gate) — it is two independent systematic
searches plus a third single-object discovery, all in 2025-2026, all using
the identical data combination (DESI DR1 RV + Gaia DR3 astrometry) this gate
was designed to test.** DESI-312 and DESI-HVS1 are the two highest-priority
front-filter exclusions if this lane were pursued further (their Gaia
source_ids were not resolved from the abstracts alone; a full-text pull would
be needed before any candidate list is finalized — flagged as an action item,
not done here since the gate stops short of a GO).

## 4. Verdict and reasoning

**NO_GO** for the lane as framed (a novel-discovery search for uncatalogued
extreme-velocity/HVS stars in DESI DR1).

Reasoning against the stated decision rule:
- Technical funnel: **passes** — 379 robust, quality-screened, novel-vs-our-
  front-filter, multi-epoch-consistent extreme-|RV| stars exist. This is not
  the failure mode.
- Confirmability: **partially passes** — the heliocentric-RV-first framing is
  archive-confirmable with no telescope (as designed), though the majority of
  individual stars can't be resolved to bound/unbound without better distances
  (Gaia DR4 or deeper follow-up parallax).
- **Prior art: fails decisively.** The lane's entire strategic rationale —
  "DESI reaches fainter than existing Gaia-RVS HVS searches, so there's a
  faint-HVS niche" — is **the exact stated rationale of two already-published
  2025-2026 papers** (Cavieres+2026 says so explicitly), one of which already
  found and published a strong HVS candidate at G≈17.1, squarely inside our
  target magnitude band. A third group (Deng+2026) independently found a
  second one. This is a saturated, actively-contested 2-3-group race, not an
  open niche — the opposite of the dark-companion RV-variability lane's
  "single-object note" finding from earlier today.
- Our own 379-star robust pool almost certainly contains DESI-312 and/or
  DESI-HVS1 and/or other stars these teams have already vetted and rejected
  (they had DESI-collaboration-internal access to more RV epochs / better
  quality cuts than our public per-healpix FITS pull) — pursuing this without
  first fully absorbing both papers' methods and candidate-rejection lists
  would very likely rediscover already-published or already-rejected objects,
  at a false-GO cost of weeks (the risk this gate was designed to prevent).

## 5. Recommendation

Do not open this as a "systematic HVS discovery" lane. If any residual value
is worth extracting cheaply:
- A **narrow, fast follow-up** confined to actually reading the Cavieres+2026
  and Deng+2026 full-text methods sections (their exact velocity cuts, epoch
  counts required, quality flags used) to see whether they exhaustively swept
  ALL of DESI DR1 or only a subset — if a real completeness gap exists (e.g.
  they used only main-survey `bright`, and our 379-star pool has resolvable
  weight in `dark`/`backup`), a narrowly-scoped "completeness gap-fill" lane
  could still have honest EV. This was NOT resolved here (full-text access
  needed) — flagged as a fast, cheap follow-up check, not a reason to reverse
  this gate's verdict on its own.
- Otherwise, the extreme-velocity pivot is exhausted at the gate stage, same
  disposition as the rest of the project's archival lanes.

## Known tool/data gotcha hit during this gate (fixed inline, not upstream)

Round-tripping `source_id` through a CSV without an explicit string dtype
truncates the 19-digit Gaia id to float64 precision (silently produces a
*different*, wrong 16-17-digit id) — this is the exact pitfall
`CLAUDE.md` already warns about ("Gaia source_ids are 19-digit — always
strings — a float truncates them"), reproduced here because
`robust_annotated.csv`'s first write used `pandas.to_csv` on a float column.
Fixed by re-deriving from the parquet (native string dtype) rather than
round-tripping the lossy CSV. `robust_annotated_v2.csv` is the corrected file;
`robust_annotated.csv` / `robust_extreme_rv_candidates.csv` are the stale,
float-truncated versions — do not reuse them if this lane is ever revisited.
