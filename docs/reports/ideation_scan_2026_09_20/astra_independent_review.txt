**Depth and coverage arithmetic:**

- At \(r=18.5\), limits of 20.5–21.3 yield disappearance thresholds of approximately **2.0–2.8 mag**. At \(r=16\), they yield **4.5–5.3 mag**. Those are useful, but the completeness function changes sharply across your parent.
- Zero detections in 3,000 trials gives a rough 95% upper limit of **\(3/3000=10^{-3}\)**, not \(10^{-5}\). With imperfect recovery, the underlying-population bound is weaker.
- Your control requirement may fail on supply. Twenty million stars over 22,000 deg² is approximately 909 deg⁻² across 2.5 magnitudes. Under a crude uniform magnitude distribution, a 3′ radius contains only **about three** stars in a one-magnitude interval, before quadrant and quality cuts—not ten.
- Requiring a detected shoulder at least one magnitude faint rejects some of the abrupt, completely censored eclipses being advertised.
- The Gaia bright-parent count is not the number satisfying the ZTF coverage, reference, isolation and duration requirements.

**Fastest cheap kill:** Run a fixed injection-and-control experiment across representative latitude, crowding and edge-distance bins. Inject both abrupt disappearances and gradual fades at the actual usable epochs. Measure recovery and image-confirmed false flags per million stars. Kill scaling if the required controls are unavailable or residual false flags overwhelm a plausible rare population.

**Novelty 3; labor 5.** This is genuinely different from the closed two-detected-state search. The known-variable duty-cycle census, however, does not satisfy your new-object objective. Do not use it to justify continuing a discovery lane that fails.

**Fastest cheap kill:** Apply the corrected cuts to real DR3 sampling with injected dips across depth and duration, including deliberately removed faint-state observations. If the TAP prefilter rejects the populations you claim to target, stop claiming a comprehensive search.

**3. SPHEREx Galactic-plane engine — NO_GO**

It already combines the same mission and preceding IR archive for the same broad eruptive populations. It is not proof that the proposed new-source sweep is exhausted, but it removes the claim that specialists are overlooking the opportunity. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07129)

**“Why not already done”: mostly self-serving.** IRSA now documents a Tractor-based forced-photometry tool with deblending, plus a source-discovery tool. “Nobody has built forced photometry” is obsolete. I did not verify an existing census identical to branch A. [Spectrophotometry tool](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/onlinehelp/spherex/spherex/sp.html), [SPHEREx archive tools](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/Missions/spherex.html)

**Depth and coverage arithmetic:**

- \(W1=15\) and \(W2=13.5\) Vega correspond to approximately **17.7 and 16.8 AB**. Broad WISE-band brightness does not guarantee three individual SPHEREx spectral-channel detections.

- The isolation cut removes precisely the crowded population used to motivate the large target count. Fainter neighbors and diffuse line emission still contaminate the survivors.

The Paschen-alpha opportunity is real, but “first survey” needs careful scope: previous space-based Paα surveys exist, including MIRIS. [MIRIS survey description](https://academic.oup.com/pasj/article/70/4/76/5039117)

**Fastest cheap kill:** Extract both-pass spectra for a small, representative set of known emitters and nonemitters spanning K and extinction. Apply the exact multi-line criteria. If recovery collapses near K=14 or nonemitters acquire comparable excesses, the advertised census is dead.

**Novelty 4; labor 5.** This is the clearest engineering trap: a survey-quality crowded-field spectrophotometry project disguised as a catalogue search. New post-NEOWISE photons distinguish it from your closed IR-nova lane, but do not solve sensitivity or validation.

**4. Deep/total-eclipsing white-dwarf binaries — NO_GO**

**Strongest prior-art threat: the exact September paper you flagged.** Its Appendix A.1 says the negative-alert sample first yielded approximately **500 periodically eclipsing white dwarfs**, to be presented separately. It included negative alerts without an additional real/bogus cut. Your proposed northern hard gate fails. [van Roestel et al. 2026, Appendix A.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.08923v1#A1)

**“Why not already done”: contradicted for the north.** The authors did not simply ignore minute-scale periodic eclipses because the paper’s headline concerns long obscurations. Excluding already recognized eclipsers from the final debris sample is not excluding them from discovery.

**Depth and coverage arithmetic:**

- At \(G=20.8\), many blue WDs are close to ZTF’s single-epoch threshold. You cannot assume 300 epochs with \(p_i>0.98\).
- At \(p=0.98\), 300 normal opportunities already produce **six expected misses**. Six misses alone are not exceptional.
- For a 1% eclipse duty cycle and 300 effectively random opportunities, the expected eclipse count is three; the probability of at least six is only about **8%**. Cadence aliases and other cuts reduce recovery further.
- Requiring misses in two quadrants sacrifices sources lacking useful overlap.
- In DES, “back-to-back” filters are not simultaneous. For minute-scale eclipses, the measured separation and exposure duration determine whether both can sample totality.
- Two faint observations in different filters do not establish a periodic eclipsing binary or distinguish WD+dM from other variability.

**Fastest cheap kill:** The northern literature test already killed the stated premise. For the southern rescue, measure the number and timing of usable close exposure pairs around the actual WD parent, then inject minute-scale eclipses. Do this before searching photometric outliers.

**Novelty 3; labor 5.** A carefully demonstrated residual selection gap might remain, but “beyond every published search” is not defensible. Do not silently repackage this as an extension of the already-running EB search.

**5. Southern DECam dwarf-nova census — GO_CANDIDATE, Stage 1 only**

**Strongest prior-art threat:** Pursiainen et al. (2018), *Rapidly evolving transients in the Dark Energy Survey*. Contrary to the proposal, its sample included **nine apparently hostless events**. A hostless fast transient is not automatically unexplored territory. [Paper](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/481/1/894/5086083)

I did not establish a dedicated, exhaustive published CV census of the complete 31,636-object DiffImg release.

**“Why not already done”: plausible for overlooked CV interpretation; overstated for unnoticed events.** The full DiffImg sample really is public. The multiseason rejection belongs to downstream selections: it does **not** remove those sources from the entire released DiffImg set. That supports using the full release, but the proposal conflates different processing stages. **Depth and coverage arithmetic:**

- Deep coadds can reveal \(r_q=21–24.5\) counterparts, while an outburst may be measurable in individual exposures. This is a sensible use of different depths because the variability evidence itself comes from the time series.
- Your assumed surface density gives **5.4–16.2 CVs** in 27 deg². Multiplying by 70% gives **3.8–11.3 faint CVs present**, before outburst probability, seasonal coverage, detection efficiency and prior discovery. It does not yield 3–7 new discoveries.
- For recurrence time \(T\), the chance of an outburst during effective monitoring time \(B\) is approximately \(1-e^{-B/T}\), before cadence losses. Calendar baseline is not continuous monitoring time.
- A quiescent object below the NSC single-exposure threshold may have no quiescent measurements. Its catalogue median can describe the outburst. Thus `median r=21.5–24.5` is not a reliable extension parent.
- Faintness and long recurrence do not establish a period bouncer. Hostlessness does not establish a halo distance. Those claims need additional evidence.

**Fastest cheap kill:** Run a recurrence/large-excursion triage on the entire small DiffImg release, vet the strongest approximately 50 objects, and check prior CV/transient identifications. If nothing remains beyond known objects, artifacts and ordinary extragalactic transients, close Stage 1; do not automatically escalate to 67.8 billion NSC measurements.

**Novelty 2; labor 2.** This is the most bounded experiment. It might find a previously unrecognized CV, but an already published DES transient renamed without new interpretation would be propagation.

**6. Reference-poisoned outbursters — GO_CANDIDATE**

**Strongest prior-art threat:** Dhawan et al. (2022), *The Zwicky Transient Facility Type Ia supernova survey: first data release and results*. Reference contamination is an established problem. I do not know a published population-wide search using precisely your reference-window selection. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07256)

**“Why not already done”: credible selection axis, exaggerated blindness.**

Difference flux is
\[
\Delta F(t)=F(t)-F_{\rm ref}.
\]

A later outburst disappears only to the extent that its flux matches the **actual reference flux**. A coadd containing a mixture of quiescent and outburst epochs generally does not equal the peak. A brighter recurrence can still generate positive alerts; fading produces negative ones.

**Depth and coverage arithmetic:**

- Reference-window start and end dates do not identify which exposures carried most weight or survived clipping.
- A one-magnitude change is a factor of 2.512 in flux. A reference substantially below the high state leaves substantial positive residual flux on recurrence.
- Requiring at least twenty outside-window detections rejects objects whose quiescence falls below ZTF’s threshold—the most strongly censored systems.
- DR24 does not necessarily contain all exposures contributing to a reference. The relevant program-dependent release selection matters.
- Joining only field/quadrant/filter without checking reference identity and history can attach the wrong reference to the claim.
- The early-2018 SN yield requires survey exposure and event-rate integration. “About 300 in reference windows” is not established by the window count.

**Fastest cheap kill:** For known contaminated controls and a small bright candidate sample, retrieve the actual reference flux and contributing-exposure information. Predict the positive and negative residuals. If “bright during the window” does not reliably predict suppressed positive alerts, your proposed selection is not selecting the advertised mechanism.

**Novelty 3; labor 3.** Survives as a targeted reference-flux anomaly search. The familiar lesson from J075308 motivates it but does not validate its population yield.

**8. 2MASS-anchored near-IR changes — MARGINAL overall**

**Strongest prior-art threat:** Pelisoli et al. (2024), *A Survey for Radio Emission from White Dwarfs in VLASS*. **Depth and coverage arithmetic:**

- At 195 μJy rms, a nominal 5σ radio threshold is approximately **0.98 mJy**. A source emitting 5 mJy at distance \(d\) reaches that threshold around \(2.3d\), assuming unchanged luminosity, frequency behavior and state.

- For illustration, at stellar density \(10^4\) deg⁻², a 2″ matching circle has about a **1%** random-match probability. Across thousands of targets, that is substantial contamination.
- A sub-day orbital period plus a WD component plus radio emission does not establish a WD pulsar. The exceptional claim usually needs coherent timing or other discriminating evidence.

**Fastest cheap kill:** Run the exact crossmatch on true and several shifted positions, then inspect multi-epoch radio morphology and astrometric quality for the strongest apparent excess. If the real sample does not exceed the false-association controls after those checks, stop.

The biggest traps are **Lane 3**, where the extraction/calibration burden is concealed by attractive line physics; **Lane 4 north**, where the asserted untouched population has already been explicitly searched; and **Lane 10’s gamma-ray branch**, where foreground associations can masquerade as exceptional Galactic systems.

**Does the project refute the July “propagation only” conclusion?**

**It refutes the categorical version, provided the EB was genuinely previously unrecognized. It does not refute a low-yield assessment of archival discovery overall.**

Taking your reported results at face value:

- **The shallow short-duty EB is the strongest counterexample.** Recovering a previously unrecognized eclipse and deriving its behavior from existing measurements is discovery. A familiar object class does not make the work propagation.
- **The probable turn-on AGN is promising evidence, not yet an established astrophysical discovery.** Demonstrating an overlooked optical state change is one result; proving AGN identity, redshift and a physical turn-on is another. Broker absence supports neither classification nor novelty by itself.
- **The CV candidate can be a new variable before it is a confirmed CV.** A secure, previously unrecognized 4.8-mag change is new knowledge. Its proposed physical class remains conditional.

The July conclusion conflated **photons already measured**, **phenomena already recognized**, and **recognized phenomena not yet registered**. Only the last is necessarily propagation. Your EB appears to occupy the second gap: the signal existed, but its meaning had not been extracted.

However, three selected successes, two still provisional, following many closed lanes do not establish a rich remaining discovery surface. They are entirely consistent with **sparse discoveries concentrated in specific selection failures, surrounded by many null searches**.

The corrected conclusion is: **propagation is more predictable; discovery remains possible but requires a demonstrated selection gap, enough surviving population, and archive-only evidence strong enough to finish the claim.** Your results justify rejecting “discovery is over.” They do not justify accepting the yield forecasts in these ten proposals.
