# DRAFT TNS AstroNote — DO NOT SUBMIT (user files personally)

Title: Rubin/LSST alert 170587115976392822 is the ongoing high state of the
uncatalogued 8-year, 5-magnitude-amplitude variable ZTF19abxfaon — likely
cataclysmic variable, not AGN

Authors: A. Keur (independent)

Abstract/body:

We report the archival identification of Rubin/LSST diaObject 170587115976392822
(RA 326.82833, Dec -13.4747; flagged by Fink as an extragalactic <20 mag candidate
and classified AGN (p=0.88) by the ALeRCE LSST stamp classifier, 2026-07)
with ZTF19abxfaon, an alert source with 491 detections spanning MJD 58732-61235
(2019-09-06 to 2026-07-14) that appears in no catalogue (TNS, VSX, SIMBAD all
negative; no Gaia DR3 source within 10").

Archival forced/catalog photometry (DECam/NOIRLab Source Catalog DR2, ZTF DRs,
PS1 DR2) shows: deep quiescence at r=23.1-23.4 (MJD 56511, 2013-08-07),
g=23.3-23.5 (MJD 57956/57987, 2017), z=22.3 (MJD 57613, 2016); PS1 2010-2014:
zero epoch detections. The source turned on between MJD 57987 (2017-08-22) and
MJD 58285.400 (2018-06-16, ZTF r=21.24+/-0.26; first r<=20.5 at MJD 58363.27) and
has since cycled between bright (r 18.0-19.5; peak r=17.98 at MJD 59218.078,
2021-01-04) and intermediate (r 20.5-21.7; 2022, 2025) states, never returning
to quiescence. Total amplitude ~5.2 mag.
The 2026-07 Rubin alert (i~18.9, 15-d rise with flickering) is the latest
re-brightening.

The counterpart is a point source in Legacy Surveys DR10 (ls_id 10995383192785753,
0.04" from the Rubin position; E(B-V)=0.04, b=-45 deg). There is no radio (NVSS,
FIRST, VLASS QL), no mid-IR (CatWISE2020/AllWISE null; LS DR10 forced W1=22.1),
and no UV counterpart. The apparent extreme i-band excess in the LS DR10 coadd
SED (i=20.8 vs r=23.1) is variability aliasing: the i coadd epochs post-date the
2018 turn-on. A deep period search (1,687 de-trended ZTF forced-photometry epochs
over 8.1 yr; Lomb-Scargle 30 min - 2 d with bootstrap thresholds, BLS eclipse
search 1.4-48 h, injection-recovery calibration) finds no coherent periodicity:
any modulation with semi-amplitude >~0.06 mag at hour-scale periods is excluded,
as are eclipses — consistent with a low-inclination system.
Two DECam exposures 13 min apart (MJD 58372.098/58372.107, r=23.1/22.0, vs ZTF
r~20.3 days before/after) suggest possible deep brief dips, pending pixel-level
verification.

The absence of any IR/radio/X-ray counterpart, the stable 4+ year deep quiescence
followed by an abrupt ~5 mag turn-on, and 8 years of state cycling with rapid
flickering favor a Galactic cataclysmic variable (VY Scl- or Z Cam-like behavior)
on a halo sightline over an AGN or supernova interpretation; the ALeRCE ZTF
forced-photometry light-curve classifiers independently rank CV/Nova at 0.95-0.97
for ZTF19abxfaon, while the AGN/SN labels come from single-stamp classifiers and
appear to be misclassifications. The source is bright (i~18.9) and active as of
2026-07-14; a single spectrum would settle the classification. Machine-readable
photometry (846 epochs, MJD-keyed, 2013-2026) is available on request.
